# Pre-explosion SMP noise control: support audit and frozen design

> Supporting research note. Scientific findings and limitations are preserved from the recorded investigation; historical result links identify the evidence available at that time. See the [study guide](../../README.md) for current execution status.

Status on 2026-09-26: **metadata-only design; no new baseline flux values or noise scores inspected.** A small released SMP pre-explosion cohort exists, so the test is not wholly unavailable. It is insufficient for a matched real/simulation noise-correlation calibration: the existing P21/G10 simulations contain no epochs in the primary window, and the provenance of the unusually early SMP measurements needs a source gate. This protocol freezes a bounded descriptive measurement and specifies what additional inputs would make it an independent noise test. It does not repeat the earlier DIFFIMG analysis.

## Existing work and the observation-level limitation

The earlier [observation-assumptions audit](snana-assumptions.md) already measured DIFFIMG off-season fluxes, fitted baselines on separate observing seasons, and retained residual scatter and temporal structure after that subtraction. It explicitly did not establish an SMP error or cosmological bias. Its SMP `abs(observer phase)>200` secondary mixes pre-peak and late-time points; late transient flux need not be zero. There were no SMP observations beyond 365 observer days from the published peak in that selected sample. Those are prior results, not new tests here.

The [release paper, section 2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05046) describes fitting free transient flux within 40 days before and 300 days after a DiffImg estimated peak, and imposing zero transient flux on reference images outside that interval to constrain the galaxy. The cited passage does not explicitly label its time frame; we do not silently interpret it as rest or observer days. The released HEAD/clump peak is not established as the original peak used to choose those scene-model images. Reference images whose flux is fixed to zero cannot validate a zero-mean flux-noise model. Sections 2.5–2.7 describe marginal SMP, source and host uncertainty, nightly averaging, and applied host-surface-brightness/chromatic/DCR corrections. Their effects must not be applied twice.

The original PHOT table supplies MJD, band, field, exposure and CCD identifiers, PHOTFLAG, flux/error, PSF, sky, gain and zeropoint metadata. The format README (`sources/repos/des-science__DES-SN5YR@1.3/0_DATA/README.md`; historical local reference) specifically identifies `SKY_SIG_T` as a DIFFIMG template-noise quantity, not an SMP template covariance. Neither PHOT nor HEAD contains a per-epoch free-versus-fixed SMP flag, the original scene-fit peak and window, galaxy/position posterior, exact reference-image membership, or cross-epoch SMP covariance. PHOTFLAG bit 1 occurs on many published accepted epochs, so it cannot simply be declared a fixed-zero or unusable indicator. Its per-row semantics have not been independently reconstructed here.

## Frozen metadata support

Inputs and code are inventoried under [offseason_noise_design](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/tree/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/astra_design/offseason_noise_design). `metadata_support.py` accesses only enumerated cadence, recorded redshift, measured peak and quality columns. It does not access FLUXCAL, FLUXCALERR, SIM_MAGOBS, SIM_PEAKMJD or other truth columns. The independent `freeze_support.py` maps every candidate to a unique absolute PHOT row and verifies its phase and flag. Reading a FITS record container loads the table storage; no numerical flux fields were accessed or summarized by these scripts.

Define phase as `(MJD − PKMJDINI)/(1 + REDSHIFT_HELIO)`, using the saved common classifier's measured clump peak, option 16 with mask 1016. The real source is the original release `DES-SN5YR_DES_{HEAD,PHOT}.FITS.gz`; simulation sources are `PH2_pilot02_{P21,G10}_{HEAD,PHOT}.FITS`. No classifier time window is applied to these raw epochs. The primary object population is the existing 1,635 DES Hubble objects; the existing 1,020 validation objects and its unchanged 1,006-member common-classifier intersection are reported separately.

The epoch quality variant uses `PHOTFLAG & 1016 == 0`, PSF FWHM `PSF_SIG1*PIXSIZE*2.355` in [0.5,2.75] arcsec, and electron zeropoint `ZEROPT + 2.5 log10(GAIN)` in [30.5,100], with the recorded SNANA small-gain convention. This is a declared diagnostic mask, not a claim to reconstruct the original SMP extraction mask. All-metadata support and mask-32 plus the same image cuts are retained in `support.json`. No signed-flux, SNR, residual, fit-chi-square or outlier cut is used.

The 1,020 and 1,006 cohorts have identical support here. Hubble primary records occupy 73 distinct observing nights and 112 object–band groups; after removing one constant per group there are at most 205 dimensions. The common subset has 50 nights, 74 groups and 101 dimensions. These are algebraic dimensions, not independent-noise counts. One object supplies 48 rows, 15.1% of the Hubble support and 27.4% of the common subset. There are only seven adjacent same-band gaps longer than 60 observer days in the Hubble primary and three in the common subset; none in the pre−100 sensitivity. Night is defined as `floor(MJD−0.5)`, putting the boundary at 12:00 UTC. Every primary object–band–night is unique, so no averaging or duplicate removal is needed in this frozen window.

Before quality cuts there are 323 pre−60 records on 39 objects and 178 pre−100 records on 36 objects. Their exact IDs, bands, dates, flags and absolute PHOT row numbers are frozen in `frozen-pre60-rows.csv`. All 175 common-subset primary rows remain before −60 using either HEAD peak or published fitted peak. Across the full Hubble primary, 271/317 satisfy that all-three-peak criterion, which is a declared peak-provenance sensitivity rather than a reason to change the primary. One object has a 57.93-day HEAD/clump difference; the maximum clump/published-fitted difference across the 39 objects is 14.11 days. `early-object-peak-agreement.csv` preserves all cases. The published fit peak is only an alignment sensitivity; it is not used to define the primary.

The simulation inputs explicitly set `GENRANGE_TREST: -40 100`. Measured clump errors allow some simulated records to appear earlier than −40, but the minima over all 2,678 P21 and 3,085 G10 written objects are only −56.76 and −54.24 days. There is no measured support at the proposed primary or stricter threshold. Loosening the threshold to −30 would give support (10,089 quality SMP rows on 1,000 Hubble objects; common subset 6,863/668), but would change the scientific control. Those metadata counts do not demonstrate that true flux at −30 is zero. No such flux score is authorized by this protocol.

## Frozen descriptive estimands if the parent executes the next step

Keep the Hubble primary and all specified sensitivities separate. First verify each frozen row's identity and finite positive quoted error, retain and report all representation failures, and report exact-zero/missing-value encodings before interpreting a distribution. No residual-dependent exclusions, threshold tuning or replacement objects are allowed. Source-confirmed free flux is required for a measurement-noise conclusion; otherwise any numeric result remains a descriptive statistic of this released table. The unavailable free/fixed metadata is not inferred from a convenient flux distribution.

1. **Baseline offsets, separate from fluctuations.** For each object and band, report the inverse-quoted-variance flux mean `b = sum(F_i/s_i²)/sum(1/s_i²)`, its formal error `1/sqrt(sum(1/s_i²))`, and `mean(F_i/s_i)`. Average the last quantity equally across bands present within an object, then equally across objects. Also retain flux-unit b and the complete object/band ledger. Its formal error is a reference under a diagonal model, not an established uncertainty. A common additive host/template offset contributes differently to F_i/s_i when quoted errors vary; an uncentered standardized product is not a pure correlation coefficient. Detection, eventual Hubble inclusion and classifier selection can produce nonzero selected baseline means even when astrophysical transient flux is zero. Baseline mean therefore does not identify a calibration error.

2. **Within-object same-band fluctuations.** Sort each group by night and use every adjacent distinct-night pair, retaining actual gaps. The primary baseline-free quantity is `D_ik=(F_i−F_k)/sqrt(s_i²+s_k²)` and its squared value, averaged first within object/band and then equally across objects. A constant additive flux baseline cancels exactly. Report separate fixed observer-gap bins (0,1], (1,10], (10,60], and >60 days; do not pool sparse bins selectively. Under a constant mean, `E[D²]=(C_ii+C_kk−2C_ik)/(s_i²+s_k²)`. Thus departures from one conflate marginal error scale and temporal covariance unless one is externally known. No division by a fitted baseline variance or comparison to simulator REDCOV as a total-flux coefficient is justified.

3. **Projection-corrected covariance diagnostic.** For each n>=2 group form `x_i=F_i/s_i`, `a_i=1/s_i`, and an orthonormal row matrix Q with Q a=0. Then y=Qx eliminates an arbitrary constant flux baseline; the diagonal quoted-noise reference is Cov(y)=I. Report `sum(y²)` and n−1, plus the fixed-lag quadratic moments. For lag ell define H_ell with off-diagonal entries `1/2` for symmetric pairs in the lag bin and zero otherwise; the centered reference score is `yᵀ Q H_ell Qᵀ y − tr(Q H_ell Qᵀ)`. This trace subtraction handles correlations introduced by estimating the baseline and unequal quoted errors. These are projected covariance departures, not estimates of the unprojected photon-noise correlation or reference-image covariance. A common constant-flux covariance component is annihilated by this projection and is therefore not identifiable. Jointly reporting the diagonal-scale score avoids mislabelling overdispersion as correlation. Use all fixed-lag pairs for this quadratic, while the variogram uses adjacent pairs; do not confuse their dependence or sample counts.

Report raw per-object contributions, field totals and leave-one-field-out point estimates. Object-cluster bootstrap intervals may be supplied only as descriptive resampling intervals conditional on the recorded fields and extraction. Shared exposures/host/reference/calibration errors prevent treating object or field sign flips as an exact physical null. Eight fields, few paired objects, and highly unbalanced cadence make asymptotic universal significance claims inappropriate. Do not convert a tail probability or score into a cosmology claim.

Frozen sensitivities are phase <=−100; the unchanged common1,006 subset; and the intersection satisfying <=−60 under all three saved peak definitions. Include the quality-mask-32 and all-record metadata ledgers for transparency; do not search masks for an attractive score. No post+100-day zero-flux assumption, SALT extrapolated mean, released-distance covariance, observer-template coefficient or new noise rescaling enters this test.

## What would make the next test scientifically informative

The minimum source gate for the current 39-object cohort is the original SMP epoch-level search/reference/free-flux assignment, original DiffImg peak/window and time-frame convention, the mapping of constituent exposures into released nightly rows, and flag meanings. The minimum input for separating common host/reference uncertainty from epoch noise is the joint scene-model/position flux covariance or a controlled re-extraction that holds out the evaluation epochs from the galaxy/reference fit. A fixed-zero reference flux, DIFFIMG replacement, or a covariance inferred from these same few residuals is not a substitute.

If those source products cannot be recovered, a useful new control requires explicitly fitting free amplitudes at pre-explosion dates using independently constrained host/reference information, preserving the exposure, PSF and zeropoint lineage. Raw public images alone do not provide that closure. The earlier detector-image acquisition is a starting inventory, not an existing SMP reproduction.

Matched simulations would need noise-only pre-explosion measurements on the same cadence and host/background support, with the same detection, measured-peak and classifier selection applied to the complete light curve, and the intended shared reference/host noise model recorded. Extending the SALT surface beyond its phase domain or appending independent Gaussian draws to the saved selected PHOT table would not reproduce this selection-conditioned measurement process. Existing P21/G10 runs cannot answer that question by changing a downstream phase cut.

The immediate recommendation is therefore to treat any execution on the frozen 317 rows as a small conditional diagnostic and pursue the epoch extraction provenance in parallel. It cannot replace the separate REDCOV intervention, establish a universal noise correction, or validate a corrected cosmology.
