# CSP J-filter interpretation: independent source review

> Supporting research note. Scientific findings and limitations are preserved from the recorded investigation; historical result links identify the evidence available at that time. See the [study guide](../../README.md) for current execution status.

The released RAISIN observation operator unambiguously assigns `J` to the old RetroCam RC1 throughput and `j` to RC2. The CSP DR3 paper groups **WIRC J with RC2**, while keeping RC1 separate. The original DR3-to-SNooPy naming therefore needs versioned interpretation: SNooPy changed the meaning of its `J` token in January 2018. This is a concrete source-level inconsistency to investigate through the already frozen row lineage and a native sensitivity calculation. It is not, by itself, a measured distance correction.

The initial source-only review ran no observed magnitude comparison, distance fit or new spectral integral; it compared filter bytes and released throughput. The later explicitly frozen historical native pilot below adds numerical validation and a bounded two-object processing response. The source snapshots, Git history, acquisition records and [offline checks](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/source-check.json) are preserved under [csp-filter-interpretation](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/tree/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation).

## What the paper and archive define

Section 6.2.3 of [Krisciunas et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05146v2) adopts one natural calibration system for WIRC J and RC2, based on their similar stellar colour terms, and another for RC1. WIRC H and RetroCam H share a natural system; WIRC Y remains separate. Equations 33–35 give `J_RC1=J_P98−0.039(J−H)_P98`, `J_RC2=J_P98−0.015(J−H)_P98`, and `H_RC=H_P98+0.030(J−H)_P98`; WIRC uses the latter J/H equations. These are standard-star calibration transformations. They do not demonstrate that arbitrary supernova spectra have identical synthetic magnitudes through the two physical filters. The archived README independently defines SN_photo `J` as pre-15-January-2009 Swope/RetroCam, `Jrc2` as the later filter, and `Jdw` as du Pont/WIRC. The [author-uploaded paper text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319875695_The_Carnegie_Supernova_Project_I_Third_Photometry_Data_Release_of_Low-redshift_Type_Ia_Supernovae_and_Other_White_Dwarf_Explosions) was also checked directly.

The distinction matters: merging Hdw into H follows the paper's declared approximation. Merging Jdw into a token subsequently interpreted as **RC1** does not follow its RC2/WIRC grouping. Small residual SED-dependent H differences can still exist without making the H naming contradictory.

## SNooPy J changed meaning

The last filter-registry change before October 2017, commit `5576598c4603c48fe195c4901f572671feed785f` (6 October 2016), has `J → J_SWO_TAM_scan_atm.dat`, with no `Jrc2` entry. Its curve bytes are exactly the pinned 2018 `Jrc2_SWO_TAM_scan_atm.dat`, SHA256 `a2769e3129803a478c90b8a3a1eb1e3e6d637fcce5ec607828a126d17c167264`. See the [historical registry](https://github.com/obscode/snpy/blob/5576598c4603c48fe195c4901f572671feed785f/snpy/filters/filters/LCO/Swope/filters.dat).

Commit [`c95928e059145b1f7f8ea88985b49a23d8db79b0`](https://github.com/obscode/snpy/commit/c95928e059145b1f7f8ea88985b49a23d8db79b0), dated **8 January 2018**, explicitly renames that registry entry to `Jrc2` and inserts `J → J_old_retrocam_swope_atm.dat`, described as the older pre-2009 filter. The latter bytes equal pinned `Jrc1_SWO_TAM_scan_atm.dat`, SHA256 `3939c6b4c446c675afb69c561f5875350bc51895e632cd7a206d29f02b5c53ab`. The current source snapshot at commit `3b97ef08e48b4d535f587d19a14eb2d0ea3a7f33` retains that mapping.

This is a real namespace change, not merely a similar filename. It could explain how an older export used `J` with an RC2-like intention. It does not prove which code wrote the available DR3 export. Archive member timestamps are 9–10 February 2018, after the transition, but mtimes are packaging metadata, not execution provenance; neither those dates nor today's registry resolves the exporter version. The archive itself contains separately named `Jrc2` groups, so the complete row mapping must be considered rather than reinterpreting every `J` globally.

The current [ASCII importer](https://github.com/obscode/snpy/blob/3b97ef08e48b4d535f587d19a14eb2d0ea3a7f33/snpy/sn.py#L2576) loads the filter token and magnitudes directly. It does not automatically S-correct an imported `J` based on observation date or the hidden instrument of origin. The same source provides an explicit `scorr` operation and `merge_filters(method='scorr')`, which subtracts saved per-epoch S corrections; an alternative merge mode can leave magnitudes unchanged. These capabilities establish possible transformations, not execution of one for the DR3 archive. The archive-creation script/version and any earlier S-correction ledger remain the relevant missing evidence.

## Converter, released KCOR and native configuration

The tagged SNANA converter (`sources/repos/RickKessler__SNANA@v11_04k/util/translate_CSPDR3.py:68`; historical local reference) reads `SN*_snpy.txt`, mapping `J→J` and `Jrc2→j`. It does not accept a Jdw token. Its parser stores the input magnitude/error without a spectral correction, then applies `F=10^(−0.4(m−27.5))` and the documented one-sided flux-error conversion. It contains no spectral model, per-epoch S correction or J-to-j remapping based on original instrument.

The released KCOR input (`sources/repos/djones1040__RAISIN_DataRelease@a383c4b/kcor/kcor_CSPDR3_BD17.input:22`; historical local reference) maps CSP-J to RC1 and CSP-j to RC2, with BD17 primary magnitudes 8.4129 and 8.4192. The actual FITS `FilterTrans` columns independently match those curves: maximum difference after peak normalization is `1.11e−16` for J and `5.16e−8` for j, consistent with stored precision. This is a check on the executable data table, not only its text input. Both `K_JJ` and `K_Jj` are present. A duplicate `K_YJ` table column makes a generic Astropy read of the entire KCOR table fail; this review read only unique throughput/primary columns and headers, without changing the file.

Nominal NIR NML (`sources/repos/djones1040__RAISIN_DataRelease@a383c4b/lcfitting/REFAC_CSP_RAISIN_nir_sys.nml:45`; historical local reference) uses this KCOR, fixes shape/AV/peak, and fits `YyJjH`. The combined branch uses the same table and fits `BomngriYyJjH`; optical-only does not fit J/H. No nominal remapping restoring WIRC J is specified. The common J zero-point variant shifts both J and j by .02 mag; that shared constant is not a replacement for an instrument- and phase-dependent throughput difference.

Root's separately frozen [magnitude-lineage audit](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/tree/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/csp_magnitude_lineage) reports exact magnitude/error equality between SN_photo and the SNooPy export, including Jdw→J and Hdw→H, and exact converter flux/error reproduction on the archive-covered released objects. This review did not recompute those observed comparisons. Conditional on that independent equality check, an S correction is absent **at those two documented links**. It remains logically possible that photometry was transformed before SN_photo, but the paper's natural-system description and explicit physical filter labels must then be reconciled with an actual source/ledger; unsupported hidden correction is not an explanation to assume.

## Sign and scientific interpretation of the synthetic difference

For throughput A or B and reference spectrum s, define `N_A[f]=integral lambda T_A(lambda) f_lambda d lambda`. Natural magnitudes give

`Delta_(B−A)[f] = −2.5 log10{(N_B[f]/N_B[s])/(N_A[f]/N_A[s])} + m_B[s]−m_A[s]`.

The equal-BD17 calculation removes the final constant term, so its sign is **m_B−m_A**. Transforming data measured in B into A requires subtracting that delta. In a single-band fixed-SED distance-only illustration, fitting B data with an A prediction instead gives approximately `D_fit−D_true=Delta_(B−A)`; simultaneous filters, fitted nuisance parameters, calibration conventions and trained templates preclude assigning that sign or magnitude to the final distance without a refit. SNooPy's explicit S-correction merge uses the compatible subtract-correction convention.

The reported Hsiao/WIRC-minus-RC1 phase sensitivity of up to .0685 mag, and H sensitivity .00836 mag, are conditional throughput-shape diagnostics. They do not establish that actual supernova SEDs follow Hsiao, that a constant primary-magnitude offset is known, or that the empirical SNooPy model did not absorb part of a calibration convention during training. A scalar zero-point shift can align a reference spectrum but cannot in general remove a spectrum-dependent difference. A native Jdw→j response is therefore the appropriate bounded next processing test, with baseline, membership, mask and optimizer gates; it is not yet a corrected cosmology calculation.

## Frozen native response design

The [protocol](../specifications/experiments/filter_response/protocol.json), SHA256 `1947050a3ac68f6a995a3d5adc93390266127feb519318e4d4947d9eaf1496a2`, preserves exactly the previously selected 42 low-z CSP objects. A metadata-only join to the independently established physical-filter ledger identifies **32 objects and 188 uniquely tagged Jdw rows**. Of these, 170 rows in 31 objects lie inside the header-defined −15 to +45 rest-day window; these counts are not yet certified native accepted masks. Four rows of 2006kf remain ambiguous `J|Jdw`. The [cohort](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/cohort42.csv), exact [known relabel rows](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/known-relabel-rows.csv), and [ambiguous rows](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/ambiguous-rows.csv) were frozen without distance responses.

The primary changes only the known rows' `J` token to `j`; every magnitude, error, flux, epoch and header remains unchanged. It retains all known affected rows, including those outside the final phase window, so the native initialization pathway is not silently altered by preprocessing. The four ambiguous rows remain J in primary; a separately labelled all-four-to-j stress arm tests that discrete ambiguity on 2006kf. This adopts the paper's WIRC/RC2 approximation; it does not claim the RC2 and physical WIRC curves are identical, and it does not apply a second S correction or a compensating scalar zero-point change.

The exact [author nominal raw FITRES](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/CSP-nominal.FITRES.gz) was acquired from `output/fit_nir_sys/CSP_RAISIN/CSPDR3_RAISIN/FITOPT000.FITRES.gz` at RAISIN_cosmo commit `b888214a5cbae38ac0bf886488ce7734f5b77e87`. Its 5,296 compressed bytes match Git blob `38334aec55bf716ac35b07c408bd7e7ef15382de`; [acquisition record](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/acquisition.json) records URL and SHA256. Headers state v11_04k/CSPDR3_RAISIN; all 42 selected IDs occur among its 69 rows. Only schema, membership and version were inspected for this design. Native raw distances must be compared against this file, not the later bias/mass-corrected cosmology table.

The first baseline pilot is lexicographically first affected **2004ef** and first unaffected **2005hc**, with two isolated nominal copies, exact released NML/model/KCOR and existing v11_04k build. The source-default MW law is retained. Copies must agree bitwise; all baseline DLMAG values must match archive within .001 mag, NDOF exactly, and data chi-square within max(.01,.001×|archive Q|). These frozen engineering caps are not widened after outcomes. An amplitude-only fit still needs stable native objective and covariance iteration: actual distinct starts must be verified after initialization, or reported as erased/untested, and a native final-mask/frozen-C positive-amplitude check plus iteration-state comparison is required. Means may be rescaled only after the SNooPy DLMAG multiplicativity and clipping support check; the SALT x0 conversion is inapplicable.

The cap is 600 active seconds on one worker, starting with the two-object pilot and a logged forecast. All 42 baseline gates precede a quantitative 42-object anchor summary; failures are not removed. Every unchanged object is a zero-change control. The primary estimands are objectwise raw-distance response and unweighted means over the 32 known affected and all 42 selected objects. Holding high-z objects fixed, the raw high-minus-low contrast changes by minus the 42-object mean; this remains a pre-bias-correction processing response. Selection, bias simulations, model-training calibration and systematic covariance need coherent regeneration before a corrected cosmology calculation is supported.


## Historical instrumentation and completed two-object numerical gate

A private copy of v11_04k was instrumented without changing the shared baseline binary or pinned sources. The first port adds terminal exports of accepted rows, native model means, true initialization coordinates, total objective/prior/error terms, and full inverse covariance for every final callback. It adds no forward-model evaluation and changes no objective, covariance, mask or parameter. The later separately frozen start hook changes **only the first iteration's distance initialization after native preprocessing**, when the explicit `CSP_POSTINIT_DSHIFT` environment variable is set. With that variable absent, all fitted state is unchanged. The final binary SHA256 is `716d5c235468329e878bd56e678cfa55b2a316f333c36ec411e9a320c1770938`; the [patches, protocols, build logs and failed attempts](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/tree/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/instrumentation) are preserved.

The first compile failed because a modern-only diagnostic variable did not exist in historical source; the versioned amendment removed that output field while retaining the actual native sigma contribution. The first invocation then stopped before reading input because the historical environment-path buffer rejected the long private build path. A recorded short symlink resolves to that exact private build. Neither failure produced a fitted outcome. Default instrumented nominal FITRES science rows and entire LCPLOT are byte-identical to the unmodified binary, including after adding the inactive start hook.

The original start test was explicitly amended **before changed-filter outcomes** to use ±0.2 mag around each branch's actual post-initialization distance, rather than the archived final distance. Nominal entry centers are 35.7428562716 and 36.5285993367 mag for 2004ef/2005hc, respectively; these exceed the printed archived final values by .0238163 and .00858934 mag. Actual entry differences equal the requested ±.2 within `2.9e−15` mag. The [source proof](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/instrumentation/entry-source-proof.json) maps `FITPAR_PREP → MNFIT_DRIVER → MNPARM → MINUIT U → FCN(U)` without reseeding. A separate dummy initial callback with zeroed output coordinates returns before model evaluation. This is source evidence for the effective starts; the very first evaluated XVAL was not separately logged. Native fitted/entry vectors are double precision; NML seed variables are single precision, which is accounted for in the all-fixed probes.

The [nominal protocol/result](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/instrumentation/probes-v3/result.json) and [changed-pilot protocol/result](https://github.com/sprajs/cosmology-revalidation/blob/17487bf659fcbdeeea072221492bac14b04a0a85/runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_profile_solver_review/csp-filter-interpretation/native-design/instrumentation/changed-pilot-probes-v3/result.json) both pass the frozen numerical gates. The latter rule was frozen at SHA256 `d5ec9d9e14a5c60a5ebb72e8a064535395ac917740cd843a32a4d2848a98eae8` before its default fit: only six prepared Jdw tokens change, then ±.2 starts are tested, and all-fixed probes use the deterministic rule `float32(default D + {0,−.15,+.15})`. For nominal 2004ef, the relative Frobenius change from iteration 2 to 3 is .00409744 for **C** and .00687442 for **W=C⁻¹**. The corresponding 2005hc changes are .000379388 and .000809874. They are distinct quantities and neither is zero. Native C changes by up to roughly 13% in the ±.15 fixed-distance probes, so the analytic amplitude formula `(fᵀWy)/(fᵀWf)` certifies a **fixed-state covariance slice**, not a state-independent full likelihood. All exported prior and sigma contributions in these probes are zero; the full native data quadratic, including its actual covariance, is reconstructed.

All six default callbacks preserve the same physical measurement multisets after the six declared label changes. Final masks contain 15 and 39 epochs. Initial 2004ef has two additional epochs at about +61.77 rest-days; the released source grid spans −20 to +70, so these interpolate within its tabulated domain, while their native model-magnitude errors are about 1 mag and they are excluded by the later −15…45 fit window. Being inside a numerical grid does not validate its empirical late-time accuracy. Across all probes, model means remain positive and C is positive definite; no flux is clamped. Actual nonzero KCOR throughput endpoints, transformed to rest frame, also stay within the native declared 2900–20000 Å screen. That screen is a numerical support check, not independent spectral validation.

The default raw-distance response for this one predeclared affected object is **2004ef: +0.0009077025 mag**, with **2005hc: exactly zero**. The old/new distance values are 35.7190450393/35.7199527418. This is a conditional processing response using the paper's RC2/WIRC approximation, with no bias correction or cosmology fit. It is not a cohort estimate. Nominal data chi-square is **105.490/14** for 2004ef and **64.888/38** for the control; the changed 2004ef quadratic is 99.817/14. Exact reproduction and stable optimization do not establish goodness of fit or physical adequacy. The two Q values use their respective native covariance states and do not constitute a calibrated likelihood-ratio test.
