# What the measured galaxy light determines about age

Flexible stellar-population mixtures reproduce much of the recovered photometry while allowing widely different mass-weighted ages. This is a direct inverse-problem result: an acceptable colour fit is not sufficient to identify a precise age in Gyr. It is consistent with the separate finding that photometric ages reliably **order** galaxies along observed stellar-population sequences. Neither result establishes an additional supernova distance correction.

## Observations and physical model

We use 788 distinct SDSS spectra with finite five-band fibre fluxes and positive inverse variances, 485 Roman et al. local apertures with usable SDSS-family ugriz fluxes, and 331 selected DES hosts with calibrated DECam ugriz and VISTA JHKs measurements. These are three aperture/catalogue samples, not 1,604 guaranteed independent galaxies. Negative flux measurements remain in the likelihood. Six SDSS counterparts lack valid five-band photometry; no cut uses a fitted age or brightness residual.

The spectra are generated by pinned FSPS 4.0 / python-fsps 0.5.0 with the actual compiled **MIST/C3K_LR** libraries, a Chabrier IMF, nebular emission at gas log U = −2, and gas metallicity equal to stellar metallicity. We allow nonnegative mixtures of 14 simple stellar populations, four metallicities and 17 distinct common-screen attenuation choices: 68 nuisance cells. The attenuation law is τ(λ) = τV(λ/5500 Å)^p, with p = −0.7 or −1 and 0 ≤ τV ≤ 2. This is a finite, explicit physical model family, not all possible stellar physics.

The main age is weighted by **formed stellar mass**. Surviving-star-plus-remnant mass is a separate estimand, tested separately. Stellar ages cannot exceed the age of a flat H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.3 clock at each observed redshift. One SSP is interpolated to that ceiling in log age; the common luminosity-distance factor is absorbed into the free mass normalization. The fit therefore does not measure cosmological distances.

SDSS native fluxes and errors receive the same approximate AB conversion: Δ(u,g,r,i,z) = (−0.04,0,0,0,+0.02) mag, using the [SDSS calibration documentation](https://www.sdss4.org/dr15/algorithms/fluxcal/). Foreground attenuation is F99, Rv = 3.1 and SFD E(B−V) × 0.86. Roman uses its catalogue-declared AB calibration. Whether the approximate native-SDSS offsets were already applied is unresolved; applying those offsets is a separate sensitivity, not a silently assumed second correction. DES uses the release's already-dereddened AB fluxes and verified DECam/VISTA reference passbands, without another foreground correction. Exact coadd response curves and inter-band calibration covariance are unavailable. The DES reference curves are a documented instrumental approximation.

## Bounds without a parametric star-formation history

For a fixed dust/metallicity cell, let A contain the predicted band fluxes per formed mass, y the observed fluxes, W the inverse-error weighting, and m ≥ 0 the SSP masses. We admit mixtures satisfying

$$
\|W(Am-y)\|_2^2\leq T,\qquad
\overline{a}=\frac{a^T m}{\mathbf 1^T m}.
$$

For the statistical-error-only calculation, T is the 95th percentile of χ² with **the number of measured bands**, not a fitted residual degree of freedom or a Wilks likelihood-ratio threshold. This defines a simultaneous Gaussian mean-flux region conditional on correct fixed errors and an included physical model.

The Charnes–Cooper change of variables u = m/(1ᵀm), t = 1/(1ᵀm) gives

$$
\mathbf1^T u=1,\quad u\geq0,\quad t\geq0,\qquad
\|W(Au-yt)\|_2\leq \sqrt T\,t.
$$

Minimizing and maximizing aᵀu are second-order-cone problems. We take the union over nuisance cells, verify the reconstructed primal solution, and use outward dual endpoints with a primal–dual objective gap below 10⁻⁵ Gyr. Failed optimization is distinguished from certified infeasibility; neither a failed endpoint nor a failed model cell may silently tighten a reported bound.

For photon-counting bandpasses, the forward operator averages Fν with weight T(λ)/λ. Redshifting obeys Fν,obs = (1+z)Lν,rest/[4πDL²], equivalently Fλ,obs = Lλ,rest/[(1+z)4πDL²]. The initial observer-grid trapezoid rule agreed with native FSPS discretization to 0.000079 mag, but independent quadrature exposed errors up to **0.02542 mag** for DES responses. Native-discretization agreement was therefore insufficient. The adopted operator uses four-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature over the union of passband and redshifted stellar-spectrum knots. Independent 0.1 Å integration agrees within **1.73 × 10⁻⁷ mag** on the checked cases. All physical fits and sensitivity/injection experiments were regenerated with this operator.

## Observed compatibility and age ranges

| Sample | Eligible measurements | Compatible with statistical errors alone | Median age-region width among compatible fits | Compatible with assumed 0.03-mag per-band discrepancy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SDSS central fibres | 788 | 768 | 12.15 Gyr | 787 |
| Roman local apertures | 485 | 430 | 10.93 Gyr | 475 |
| DES eight-band hosts | 331 | 124 | 7.10 Gyr | 307 |

All finite signed fluxes are retained, including five Roman apertures whose summed flux is nonpositive. Weak nondetections admit the full age support when zero lies inside the flux region; a strongly incompatible negative measurement remains a failed physical-model check. The final full samples contain **zero unresolved numerical endpoint failures**.

The uncertainty regions concern formed-mass mean age, not supernova progenitor delay. Different redshifts impose different age ceilings, so the widths are not a controlled comparison of survey precision. A parametric SFH or population prior can narrow them, but then those restrictions contribute information beyond the fluxes. This calculation does not demonstrate that all published Bayesian age intervals have incorrect coverage under their own models.

The extra 0.03-mag term is an **assumed model/calibration sensitivity**, added in quadrature to each band error using its observed flux. It is not a measured covariance or a calibrated 95% confidence procedure. With that term the DES median width is 7.47 Gyr. Its large effect on feasibility shows why formal photometric errors alone cannot certify stellar-model accuracy. We do not tune the errors to force every galaxy to fit; 24 DES hosts still fail that sensitivity family.

An SDSS `clean` flag is retained rather than used to conceal problematic measurements: 604 of the 788 spectra have clean associated photometry. Central fibre photometry and spectroscopy share a nominal three-arcsec diameter, but their PSFs, seeing and spectrophotometric calibration differ.

## Held-out spectral information

The narrow 4000 Å break is independently integrated from the downloaded flux spectra. The forward model applies foreground attenuation at the observed wavelength and predicts the same rest-frame blue/red bands. Index comparisons require effectively complete usable coverage; every accepted ≥99.5% case in this sample has complete bands.

Among 698 photometrically compatible hosts with usable index data, **191** have disjoint photometry-predicted and measured 95% Dn4000 intervals. This is a descriptive model check, not a 95%-level rejection test: two separately valid 95% regions have a joint Bonferroni guarantee of at least 90%. Shared calibration, covariance and model errors remain relevant. Under the assumed 0.03-mag sensitivity, the mismatch count falls to 1/716. This change supports scrutiny of model and calibration precision rather than declaring a new stellar-age correction.

We also add the index as a constraint using its **Fieller interval**, constructed from the raw red/blue mean fluxes, their variances and their covariance. A ratio is not assumed Gaussian. Linear inequalities in the SSP coefficients enforce that ratio interval. Combining a 97.5% photometric region and 97.5% index region gives at least 95% joint conditional coverage by Bonferroni, without assuming the two data products are independent. Within-spectrum pixel covariance and inter-band photometric covariance remain unavailable.

With statistical errors alone, 527 hosts admit a joint model; the median age-region width is still **11.63 Gyr**. With the assumed discrepancy, 715 admit a joint model and the median width is 11.76 Gyr. One spectral index helps reject some mixtures; it does not uniquely reconstruct a star-formation history.

## Assumption and numerical tests

- A 500-draw Gaussian experiment gave 95.2% coverage for the input mean-flux region and 99.8% for its projected age interval. The latter is deliberately conservative. No feasible-input solver failures occurred.
- Twenty-four age extrema were checked using six predetermined independent primal starts each, with simplex mass fractions and a separately scaled amplitude. Every endpoint has a successful matching solution; the largest best-solution difference is **7.88 × 10⁻⁹ Gyr**. All 52 unsuccessful attempts remain recorded. An earlier coordinate formulation falsely reported success 0.01979 Gyr from the optimum; optimizer status alone is not an accuracy certificate.
- A further 240 targeted injections include a native off-grid SSP and differential dust/metallicity mixtures. The DES differential-dust case admits and covers 34/40 statistical-only realizations, versus 40/40 under the discrepancy sensitivity. These small, post-inspection tests expose model-family dependence; they are not a precision coverage certificate for every galaxy.
- A deterministic eight-object-per-sample check refines SSP ages, metallicity and attenuation grids. It preserves the broad age ranges. Surviving-mass weighting, foreground scaling and native SDSS zero points are separate sensitivities, not relabelled versions of the main age.
- A nonaccelerating Einstein–de Sitter **stellar-age clock** still admits the same number of photometric fits in the small comparison, but lowers upper age bounds by roughly 3–4 Gyr. This is not a cosmological fit or evidence favouring that model. It demonstrates that the assumed cosmic clock must be propagated if ages enter cosmological inference.

The numerical checks caught an actual solver problem before adopting the fit results. On a real historical 5 × 14 observer-grid design, SciPy 1.15.3 NNLS reports squared residual 10.68968 although its returned coefficients give **12.04871**. Column-scaled bounded-variable least squares gives **11.85549**, agreeing with SciPy 1.18.1 NNLS. The discrepancy repeats with both tested NumPy versions. New calculations use the independently checked bounded-variable solver and recompute every residual. A separate audit of older BAO and infrared designs found no occurrence of this residual inconsistency; its distinct cancelled-mass-mode finding is documented in the [galaxy validation note](galaxy-validation-results.md).

## Computation and reproduction

The forward spectra use compiled Fortran; matrix operations use BLAS; optimization uses the compiled Clarabel solver. Independent galaxies run in parallel. Batched CUDA evaluation of all 331 DES hosts on the RTX 2080 Ti agrees with CPU double precision to **1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵** and was **4.32 times faster including transfers** in the recorded benchmark. The matrix step takes 0.145 s on one CPU thread versus 0.034 s on the GPU; the small constrained optimizations dominate the complete fit. A matrix benchmark is not a claim of equal whole-program speedup. Float32 is measured but not used for the reported bounds.
