# Observed host likelihoods and the limits of an age correction

We recovered actual joint galaxy-model draws and high-redshift host spectra. They substantially improve the available observational inputs, but they do not yet identify a correction to supernova luminosity with redshift. Two independent checks fail: many public YSE light curves are inconsistent with the working light-curve/noise model, and repeated OzDES spectra are inconsistent with a common count-spectrum colour under their supplied pixel variances. These failures remain visible; neither is converted into an age correction or repaired by arbitrary error inflation.

## Joint age, dust and star-formation information

The [FrankenBlast public release](https://zenodo.org/records/16953206) contains 4,499 spectroscopic-sample host HDF5 files. Of these, 4,385 contain usable joint chains; 114 lack a posterior chain. We recovered 49,790 observed host-flux entries and their reported errors, preserving native masks. None of the released valid entries is negative; our reader imposes no positive-flux cut. The photometry is already foreground-corrected and includes the author's 1% error floor. Interband, aperture and deblending covariance is not supplied.

The native chains preserve 17 simultaneous model parameters. We derive formed-mass-weighted age, formed stellar mass, diffuse stellar attenuation and young-star attenuation from the **same chain rows**. This matters because the [author's postprocessing functions](https://github.com/anugent96/frankenblast-host/blob/94f81ed6d27a190832c891bb8452e54b0ddbff2e/postprocess_sbi.py) independently resample different exported quantities; pairing those separate arrays would invent their joint covariance.

There are 2,920 hosts labelled normal Type Ia. Among the 1,355 with association probability at least 0.9, the median within-host age standard deviation is **1.81 Gyr**. The median posterior age–diffuse-attenuation correlation is **−0.360**, and the age–formed-mass correlation is **+0.774**. These describe the released model-conditioned posteriors, not direct age measurements or an unbiased population distribution.

The ages retain the author's WMAP9 clock, seven constant-SFR intervals and empirical SBI training prior. The model uses global formed-mass-weighted age; it does not measure progenitor delay. Its stellar attenuation parameters are not the supernova's line-of-sight extinction. The training population is a mixture with additional mass–metallicity and photometric-selection structure, so dividing these posterior draws by a table of independent one-dimensional priors would not recover an exact likelihood. A changed cosmological clock or host population requires the full joint prior/selection density and adequate importance-sampling support. The recovered raw fluxes offer a separate route to independent stellar modelling.

[CIGaRS](https://github.com/snai-analysis/cigars/tree/160f823ea6e0bd0d35b0ee5428e66927db537d52) is a useful executable joint galaxy/SN model, but its published 1,578-object target is simulated. It is not an additional observed DES sample or an empirical correction prior.

## An independent brightness test that did not pass

There are 425 exact-name matches between the usable host chains and [YSE DR1](https://zenodo.org/records/7317476), including 256 normal-Ia labels. Position, host-centroid, association-probability and spectroscopic-redshift checks leave **66** candidates before fitting brightness. Of these, 62 have enough usable epochs and a converged numerical fit; 51 pass the separate sampling/parameter-quality requirements.

The released PS1 and ZTF difference fluxes use AB zeropoint 27.5. However, the archive described as having no S/N cut still removes negative flux, applies a magnitude-error cut and excludes flagged epochs. Rejected epoch times and values are absent. Our positive-truncated Gaussian fit accounts only for the known positive-flux condition; it is not the full event/epoch selection likelihood. The header's 0.005 spectroscopic-redshift error is a placeholder, not an individually measured uncertainty.

The median nominal light-curve χ²/dof is **5.25** across the 62 completed fits. Only **9/62** pass an exploratory χ² upper-tail screen of 0.01. This screen is not a selection-conditioned predictive certification. For example, SN 2019tvv has a released ZTF-g sequence near maximum of 24,768±580, 43,296±418 and 24,906±228 flux units over six rest-frame days. Its smooth-model residuals are −15.8, +27.3 and −9.8 reported standard deviations. Changing only the positive-flux normalization does not remove this problem.

We checked the implementation rather than assuming the release or the model was wrong. Forty-eight direct numerical passband integrations agree with native `sncosmo` band fluxes within **0.060 mmag**. Four independent optimizer/objective checks agree within **0.00005** negative-log-likelihood units; three terminate with a precision-loss warning, which is retained. These checks validate the calculations, not the physical light-curve model. Template uncertainty, extraction artefacts, calibration and object classification remain possible contributors.

The low-S/N magnitude metadata do not universally match the elementary logarithmic transformation of FLUXCAL; 16 of the 6,407 candidate-source epochs have negative MAGERR. We retain examples and do not reinterpret them as measured negative uncertainty or declare photometric errors: nondetection/sentinel conventions need clarification. The paper's explicit luptitude discussion concerns host photo-z inputs, not an established conversion for these SN-epoch columns. No magnitude-derived cut is silently substituted for the released flux data.

The signed-data recovery attempt checked the current official release, its earlier Zenodo record and the public YSE-PZ software/API documentation. The earlier record has no downloadable files; the model API requires authenticated access. The independent photometric fit therefore remains conditional on the selected public flux archive. **No host-age brightness coefficient is reported from this cohort.** All brightness-analysis gates remain closed, even where numerical fitting succeeds.

## High-redshift observed spectra and repeatability

The [OzDES DR2 release](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/ftp/J/MNRAS/496/19/ReadMe) supplies spectra, variance and bad-pixel arrays. The independently constructed [DES–OzDES association record](../results/survey_selection/ozdes-crossmatch.json) yields **1,089 Dovekie supernovae in 1,088 distinct spectroscopic hosts**, spanning z=0.074–1.145. The shared host enters the host likelihood once. Identity gates use host coordinates, a unique match within one arcsecond, host-target classification, secure redshift and redshift agreement; the latter is not an independent redshift validation because OzDES supplies many DES redshifts.

All 1,088 hosts have at least 80% unmasked coverage of the rest-frame 3850–3950 and 4000–4100 Å windows. We preserve signed band measurements, seven-window covariance, wavelength/mask kernels and weak measurements. Twelve blue-window and five red-window measurements are nonpositive. Under a working independent-pixel covariance, 1,049 hosts have two-sided nominal likelihood-ratio regions within the declared count-ratio grid 0–4. This is a numerical support statement, not a physical age or calibrated Dn4000 measurement. A further covariance sensitivity uses adjacent-pixel correlations 0.25 and 0.5, explicitly as assumptions.

The [official OzDES FAQ](https://docs.datacentral.org.au/ozdes/overview/faq/) says relative flux calibration is not established. The native units are normalized counts per wavelength. Fibre positioning, chromatic response and seeing affect the continuum; [release documentation](https://docs.datacentral.org.au/ozdes/overview/dr2/) also warns about discontinuities from arm normalization. We flag 290 hosts whose break windows overlap a dichroic region. A common response error is not removed by a high continuum S/N.

We measured **13,792 individual exposures**, retaining signed weak measurements. In 1,081 hosts with at least two usable exposures, a shared count-break ratio with separate nonnegative exposure amplitudes gives median χ²/dof **2.11**; 439/1,081 have a nominal upper-tail probability below 0.01. To check that weak-flux boundaries alone did not explain this, we simulated and refitted 200 Gaussian realizations for each of 40 hosts selected by a deterministic hash of their identifier. **22/40** reject the fitted common-ratio/variance model at the 0.01 level in this conditional check. This demonstrates inadequate differential-response/noise modelling for many hosts; it does not uniquely identify the instrumental cause or calibrate a shared response.

We also preserve a more local Hδ measurement: an interpolated continuum and a signed absorption contrast, with their full 2×2 covariance. The operator cancels constant and linear count continua to numerical precision. All 1,088 hosts have sufficient coverage; four continuum estimates are nonpositive and 264 absorption contrasts are negative. These are retained observations, not forced absorption detections. Response curvature, emission infill, stellar metallicity, instrumental resolution and the central aperture still matter. No noisy equivalent-width division or conversion into a stellar age is imposed.

## How these measurements enter a unified model

The [likelihood module](../code/host_likelihood/likelihood.py) accepts signed host-flux vectors with full supplied covariance and integrates conditional SN brightness over aligned host draws. It requires an explicit unchanged-host-model declaration or a full joint population density ratio. Shared SN calibration must be conditioned on explicitly; overlapping corrected distances and host residuals cannot be multiplied as independent evidence. Zero population support returns zero effective sample size and minus-infinite log likelihood.

The spectra expose pixel operators so that a physical stellar model can be projected into the same measurements with normalization, response and aperture nuisances. The useful next measurements are a calibrated spectral response tied to matched-aperture photometry, independently assessed correlated spectral noise, and a selection model that includes unsuccessful host redshifts. For the YSE brightness test, signed upstream epochs, extraction/quality information and a validated template-plus-calibration likelihood are needed before testing a conditional age coefficient.

Even an identified within-redshift age–brightness coefficient does not, by itself, determine the selected population's mean residual luminosity B(z). A distance change can be absorbed by a free grey B(z); host photometry alone does not constrain that direction. The present module therefore exports observed likelihood inputs and explicit conditional assumptions, **not an empirical B(z) prior**.

[Validation](../results/host_likelihood/validation.json) checks source/result hashes, joint-chain algebra, independent pixel sums and covariance, ratio profiles, repeat fits, light-curve diagnostics and closed scientific gates. [Reproduction commands](../code/host_likelihood/README.md) retain downloads in the ignored work directory; no source catalogue, author code archive or regenerable array is committed.
