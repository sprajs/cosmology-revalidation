## DES16 quoted-error transformation

I separately reparsed the exact Git-blob author force-photometry files and the released RAISIN products, preserving source line, band/MJD, `PHOTFLAG`, `ZPFLUX`, PSF, sky and gain columns in [raw-product-error-rows.csv](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_cadence_error_bridge_review/raw-product-error-rows.csv). Among the 3,135 positive rows in the ten DES16 raw precursors, 3,133 have a unique same-band/time product match; the two remaining `DES16C3cmy` rows share a duplicate timestamp and are left ambiguous. The product flux is within `0.0005` of raw flux for every unique row. The 73 cases failing a literal Python `round(raw_flux,3)` check lie at printed half-thousandth ties, so the flux data support three-decimal output precision rather than a fitted rescale.

The error difference is **not merely decimal rounding**: only 209/3,133 unique positive rows have `product_error = round(raw_error,3)`. All **3,133/3,133** obey the predeclared coefficient identity

\[
\mathrm{product\_error} = \operatorname{round}_{0.001}\!\left(\mathrm{raw\_error}\;1.086\,(0.4\ln 10)\right),
\qquad 1.086(0.4\ln 10)=1.0002429643966138.
\]

The unrounded residual is within ±`0.0005` in all cases, exactly the product’s three-decimal precision. This holds for every object, `g/r/i/z` band and source `PHOTFLAG` stratum, including the major groups (`PHOTFLAG=1`: 2,405/2,405; `0`: 129/129; `1025`: 216/216; `12288`: 286/286). The median absolute raw-to-product error difference is `0.0017` FLUXCAL units, 90th percentile `0.004`, and largest `0.134` (on a raw error of `551.54`); ratio variation is dominated by output rounding. No additive or flux-dependent error term is required to explain these DES16 product errors. [The grouped machine result](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_cadence_error_bridge_review/error-bridge-result.json) gives each object, band and flag distribution. The seven DES15 earlier raw precursors do **not** satisfy this identity: 0/274 uniquely matched positive rows, consistent with their known changed photometric reduction.

The multiplier has a specific algebraic interpretation. The approximate magnitude-error derivative `σ_m≈1.086 σ_F/F`, followed by the exact inverse derivative `σ_F=F(0.4 ln 10)σ_m`, gives precisely this factor. The pinned author [HST-only update script](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_sign_source/raisin_cosmo/update_phot.py) explicitly uses `0.4*np.log(10)` when converting HST magnitude errors back to flux errors; it copies DES optical rows and therefore **does not document this optical transformation’s execution**. A pinned [SNANA utility](../../sources/repos/RickKessler__SNANA/util/make_hostlib_diffsky.py) uses `1.086` as the approximate derivative, but it is also not an execution trace for RAISIN optical photometry. The pinned author source tree has no identified script that writes DES optical product rows from these raw precursors. Thus the conversion is a **numerically exact inferred operation**, not a proven code path. The [formula amendment](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_cadence_error_bridge_review/error-formula-amendment.json) recorded the unfit coefficient before the independent raw/product reparse; it was motivated by preliminary prior-ledger inspection and is labelled accordingly.

For a conditional signed-row refit, applying this factor to original DES16 negative-row quoted errors would reproduce the error convention seen on every uniquely matched positive row, while leaving their signed fluxes unchanged. That is a specific, testable bridge, not proof that the historical pipeline would have made exactly the same operation on excluded negative measurements. Preserve the two duplicate-time rows and the different DES15 revision as separate ambiguities. No fit, simulated noise, bias correction or cosmology outcome was run in this review.
