## 3. Test A: BAO no-acceleration cone, independent derivation and limits

This is genuinely additional to the local CPL prior sensitivity and SN-only q-bin optimization. `scripts/cosmology/core.py` has q-bin E(z) and distance integrals, but `BAO.prediction` hardcodes CPL; `scripts/cosmology/kinematic_limits.py` fits supernovae. The new parent implementation should remain separate and preserve these records.

Use the 12 anisotropic entries (six D_M/r_d and D_H/r_d pairs) from `sources/repos/CobayaSampler__bao_data/desi_bao_dr2/desi_gaussian_bao_ALL_GCcomb_{mean,cov}.txt`, retaining their joint covariance and original ordering. Hold out the nonlinear BGS D_V entry initially. The selected covariance is the covariance submatrix, not a principal submatrix of precision. Their redshifts are 0.510, 0.706, 0.934, 1.321, 1.484 and 2.33. Neither q(0) nor the research plan's primary 0.05–0.30 interval is resolved directly by these points.

Let t=ln(1+z), g(t)=(1+z)D_H(z)/r_d. With a constant ruler,

\[
\frac{d\log g}{dt}=-q(z).
\]

Thus no acceleration is g positive and nonincreasing. For flat geometry, d(t)=D_M/r_d=\int_0^t g(s)ds. At ordered measured points the closure obeys

\[
g_N\ge0,\quad d_1\ge t_1 g_1,\quad
\Delta t_j g_j\le d_j-d_{j-1}\le\Delta t_j g_{j-1},\ j\ge2.
\]

The interval inequalities imply sampled monotonicity. The first integral has no finite upper bound because g(0), hence the uncalibrated scale, is unconstrained. Adding a bound there would quietly add a low-redshift prior. These linear constraints form a closed convex cone. Separate checks are radial-only isotonicity and the necessary Alcock–Paczynski inequalities d_j-t_j g_j>=0; do not treat these correlated checks as independent detections.

**Sufficiency and rays.** For each measured t_k define two nonnegative step rays with d_j=min(t_j,t_k) and g_j=1[j<k] or 1[j<=k]. A step inside (t_k,t_{k+1}) has the same sampled g as the k-plus and (k+1)-minus rays; its integral vector is their convex interpolation. First-interval steps are multiples of the first-minus ray, and a constant g is the last-plus ray. Positive monotone functions are mixtures of these drops plus a constant, so the 2N rays generate the exact sampled closure. Whitened nonnegative least squares on the rays and an independent inequality-constrained quadratic solver should agree in objective, fitted vector, constraint feasibility and KKT residuals over random synthetic inputs.

An equality corner may require an instantaneous jump. The first-minus ray even has zero sampled g with positive integrated distance. These are limits of positive continuous histories with arbitrarily sharp finite transitions, not smooth histories attaining every corner exactly. Report which boundaries the fitted solution occupies, and whether smooth positive approximations approach its objective. Do not label these limits a measured expansion history.

**Calibration.** Use T(y)=min_{m in C}(y-m)^T Sigma^-1(y-m). For fixed Gaussian covariance, after whitening, C is still a convex cone. Since C+C is contained in C,

\[
\mathrm{dist}(Z+m,C)\le\mathrm{dist}(Z,C),\qquad m\in C.
\]

Consequently a zero-mean Gaussian simulation at the cone vertex gives a provably conservative upper-tail calibration for every null mean in the cone. The vertex is an unphysical limiting absolute scale, but is valid for this upper bound. Coasting at a fitted positive amplitude offers a sharper conditional calibration; it is not automatically the universal least-favourable null. Report both plus binomial Monte Carlo uncertainty. Boundary constraints invalidate an automatic Wilks chi-square conversion. Test injected coasting, positive-q, accelerating, and near-boundary histories and demonstrate projection correctness and power.

**Failure criteria.** No cosmological rejection if NNLS and independent optimization disagree materially, whitening/order is wrong, the statistic depends materially on solver tolerances, or the fixed likelihood assumption fails. A significant rejection is conditional on flat geometry, constant r_d, and the released compressed Gaussian likelihood. It does not diagnose dark energy, SN dust, or host age.

The deepest additional caveat is the **effective-redshift compression**: released D_M and D_H at z_eff are not automatically pointwise observables for arbitrarily abrupt expansion histories across a tracer's redshift window. Smoothness-free cone geometry is conservative inside the supplied likelihood, but is not by itself a raw-catalog theorem for every conceivable FLRW history. This issue is especially relevant if the best constrained fit sits at abrupt endpoint drops. Curvature also changes d'=g, while ruler evolution can mimic g's monotonicity. Make those conditions prominent.

## 6. Conditions on E05 and order of execution

It is a **conditional required-versus-admitted correction calculation**. Do not treat arbitrary-sign covariance eigenvectors as measured signed physical changes. The later BGS extension is mathematically valid as a necessary inequality under its stated assumptions. Define b=0.295, t_b=ln(1+b), and

\[
s_b=\frac{D_V(b)/r_d}{[b\,t_b^2/(1+b)]^{1/3}}
    =[\bar g_b^2 g_b]^{1/3}\ge g_j\quad(z_j>b),
\]

because \(\bar g_b=t_b^{-1}\int_0^{t_b}g(t)dt\ge g_b\ge g_j\). Both s_b and g_j are linear transformations of released measurements; no artificial separate BGS radial/transverse measurement is introduced. The parent's 28-contrast maximum retains correlations, including the shared BGS observation, and coasting attains zero for all contrast means, a least-favourable null for that fixed maximum. The strongest contrast is BGS versus z=0.934 at 4.1883 standard errors. The saved one-million-draw finite-family tail is 0.000341, with interval [0.000306, 0.000379].

This rule was selected after the anisotropic and BGS plug-in diagnostics, so the 28-contrast adjustment does not account for all adaptive research choices. It remains exploratory conditional evidence for some past acceleration under flat, constant-ruler, compressed-Gaussian assumptions. It does not replace the primary result, measure q(0), refute models with present deceleration and earlier acceleration, validate a supernova correction, or demonstrate new physics.

Independent central-value calculations in `bao_scale_budget.py` reproduce s_b=32.04368 and g_0.934=34.11865. To eliminate this one contrast requires a BGS-only D_V increase of 6.4754%, a radial-only D_H decrease of 6.0816%, or an illustrative ruler ratio r_d(0.934)/r_d(0.295)=0.939184 at coasting equality. A common absolute scale cancels. The minimum shift in the released covariance metric repairs this one contrast with BGS +2.4869%, D_H(0.934) -3.7460%, and its correlated D_M change, costing 17.5418 in that metric. These are required shifts, not empirically allowed systematics, and need not repair the whole contrast family.

There is a useful standard-curvature necessary bound. Write Omega_k=-kappa for closed FLRW geometry, and I_b=integral_0^b dz/E(z). Under no acceleration from today, E(z)>=1+z, so I_b<=t_b. On the positive pre-antipodal branch with sqrt(kappa)*t_b<pi, sinc is decreasing, hence

\[
\frac{s_b}{g_j}\ge\operatorname{sinc}(\sqrt{\kappa}\,t_b)^{2/3}.
\]

The observed central ratio requires **kappa>=8.2913** for curvature alone to evade that necessary bound. Flat/open geometry does not provide the required decrease. This is a central-value stress scale, not a curvature fit, credible interval, external prior or calibrated robustness significance. Error propagation and the other BAO observations must be treated jointly for an admitted curved-model inference. Very extreme expansion/curvature/ruler alternatives also require checking whether the released effective-redshift compression remains an adequate likelihood.
