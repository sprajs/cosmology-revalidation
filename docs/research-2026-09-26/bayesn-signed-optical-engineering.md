# Signed-optical BayeSN engineering gate

The two-object adapter passed its forward and algebra checks. This is an implementation result for DES16E2clk and DES16X3cry, not an observed-flux likelihood, posterior, NIR prediction, or test of the distance hypotheses. The [frozen design](bayesn-signed-optical-pilot.md) and [execution protocol](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/bayesn_signed_optical_pilot/execution-protocol.json) were pinned before the forward run. All outputs are in the [isolated run directory](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/bayesn_signed_optical_pilot).

The adapter keeps 15 and 14 signed author optical r/i/z rows, including their original flux and error coordinates and row identity. The 6 and 5 released J/H rows were written to a separate sealed file; the forward/algebra and benchmark runners open only the optical payload. This preparation step did parse the NIR source file to create that sealed payload, but did not evaluate those outcomes. The structural swap runner used a wholly synthetic NIR sentinel and reproduced all four forward arrays, quoted errors, and masks exactly. No optical posterior exists yet, so the sealed real NIR file must remain outside any subsequent fit, initialization, or diagnostics until those outputs are frozen.

The missing DES J adapter is the released `kcor_DES_NIR.fits` column `WFC3_IR_F125W-J` with its released AB primary magnitude and zero offsets. The new filter file, custom YAML, and source hashes are in `adapter-manifest.json`. The exact merged-knot reference magnitude differs from the model's integral by **8.58×10⁻⁷ mag**, below the 0.001-mag gate. The positive supported rest-wavelength range across the two redshifts and five r/i/z/J/H bands is 3356–12509 Å, within M20's 3000–18500 Å domain. The trigger headers equal the frozen metadata exactly; no released peak date enters the forward phase.

The forward model recomputes phase, the spline time map, and Hsiao interpolation from latent rest-frame peak offset `tau`. Both tau prior endpoints were finite with eager/JIT disagreement at most 5.03×10⁻¹⁵ quoted error. Six explicit spline-knot crossings were finite; the largest symmetric finite-step departure was 1.61×10⁻⁷ quoted error at a 10⁻⁶-day step. Hsiao's piecewise-linear interpolation has possible derivative kinks at exact integer phase; the check establishes finite, effectively continuous flux, not differentiability exactly on every crossing surface. The all-coordinate half-step derivative discrepancy away from the tested crossings was 1.53×10⁻⁹ relative; the synthetic scalar JAX gradient discrepancy was 4.38×10⁻¹⁰. At fixed AV=0, the RV flux difference was zero. A +0.15-mag gray shift changed flux by the expected `10^(-0.4×0.15)` to 3.10×10⁻¹⁵ relative precision.

Wavelength integration at 300, 600, 1200, and 2400 bins had successive maximum changes of 0.01694, 0.003307, and **0.001074** quoted error, passing the final 0.1-error gate. The proper normalized broad-distance convolution was checked with a negative *synthetic* optical flux: adaptive quadrature and an independent dense D grid differed by 2.37×10⁻¹² in the log joint-over-optical integral. The broad D density normalized to 1 over the declared encompassing integration interval. These checks used no observed optical flux in any objective.

The forward/algebra gate took 54.0 seconds under the 15-minute cap. The separately frozen synthetic-only benchmark compiled in 17.7 seconds, ran 100 gradients in 0.155 seconds, and completed five warmup transitions plus one discarded draw in 6.92 seconds; total 24.8 seconds under its five-minute cap. This very short benchmark is not a chain convergence test or a reliable full-chain runtime forecast. Full synthetic recovery, observed optical fits, and held-out NIR scoring remain unexecuted.

A later [bounded runtime protocol](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/bayesn_signed_optical_pilot/runtime-protocol.json) used a *normalized* synthetic optical Gaussian likelihood with both proper D priors. Its broad D log density (uniform μ on 20–50 convolved with the 0.088-mag gray Gaussian) matched independent μ quadrature within 6.75×10⁻¹⁴ in log density at 51 finite reference points and normalized to 1 over 15–55. Eighty warmup transitions and 20 discarded draws per arm took 11.9 seconds for LCDM and 7.7 seconds for broad after model setup; postwarmup NUTS steps had median 63, 90th percentile 139.8 and maximum 511 in each arm. The entire run took 39.9 seconds under its 180-second cap. The crude single-chain 1000+1000 projections are 238 and 154 seconds, respectively, but short adaptation, compilation, long-tree tails, four-chain mixing and all later synthetic cases remain unmeasured. The earlier toy-D quadrature checks only probability algebra; they are not a BayeSN likelihood integration or posterior check.

One requested additive check remains **not run**: J/H forward predictions at held-out metadata epochs, including 1200→2400→4800 wavelength refinement and a dynamic-versus-static phase comparison. The existing J reference-zero check does not establish that complete J/H forward operator. Work stopped on the user's request before this new gate or any full synthetic chain.

The [`forward-algebra-result.json`](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/bayesn_signed_optical_pilot/forward-algebra-result.json), [`benchmark-result.json`](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/bayesn_signed_optical_pilot/benchmark-result.json), [`synthetic-nir-swap-result.json`](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/bayesn_signed_optical_pilot/synthetic-nir-swap-result.json), source-support ledger, protocols, and [`result-manifest.json`](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/bayesn_signed_optical_pilot/result-manifest.json) retain the values and hashes. These gates permit the next separately reviewed synthetic stage; they do not authorize opening held-out NIR data for observed inference.
