# Public WFC3/IR dark-ramp metadata near the DES16E1dcx visits

The completed metadata search identifies **six search-visit and eight
template-visit full-frame SPARS50 DARK ramps**. They have16 reads, while the
science exposures have8. Exact first-eight timing, reset/history and processing
equivalence remain to be checked before a noise comparison. The earlier
zero-match result below describes only the first bounded search; the additive
continuation found this candidate cohort without examining pixels.

This is a metadata feasibility audit for a future read-noise/slope-covariance control. It reads official [MAST CAOM and product metadata](https://mast.stsci.edu/api/v0/_services.html) and byte-range FITS **primary headers only**. It downloads no image arrays and measures no dark current, read noise, or science-image residuals. The frozen query and selection rules are in `runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_hst_pixel_feasibility/dark_ramp_metadata/protocol.json`; exact queries, source IDs, response hashes, and per-root headers are preserved in the same directory.

The ±30-day public calibration/DARK query returned 386 CAOM observations around the search visit (MJD 57715.6503) and 374 around the template visit (MJD 58077.6242). The triggered ±180-day metadata tier returned 992 and 589, respectively. These are CAOM records, **not verified read-pattern matches**. RAW and IMA product records were verified for each of the nearest 40 exposures of at least 300 s per visit. Range requests obtained those 80 RAW primary headers. None had the required full-frame `SPARS50`, `NSAMP≥8` configuration: the nearest sets contain twenty `SPARS25` subarrays each, with remaining full-frame `STEP50`, `STEP100`, `STEP200`, `STEP400`, or `SPARS200` modes (exact counts in `result.json`).

An independently frozen diagnostic checked the nearest eight 352.939514-s DARKs per visit because exposure time alone did not determine their sampling mode. All sixteen are full-frame `SPARS25`, `NSAMP=16`, rather than `SPARS50`. Their dates are about 9–13 days before the science visits. The entire 96-header audit therefore finds **zero exact matches**. The broader ±30- and ±180-day records remain only CAOM metadata; resource limits preclude claiming that no suitable SPARS50 products exist there. The recorded acquisition used 283.69 of 300 allowed seconds and 4.97 of 20 MB metadata.

The verified dark products have public RAW and IMA URIs. RAW is preferable for a later paired-read study: subtracting independent RAW ramps at the same detector pixel can cancel a stable mean pattern, subject to matched reference-pixel/nonlinearity handling, persistence, and detector-state limits. IMA/FLT residuals may reuse a master `DARKFILE` trained on some of these calibration darks, so their agreement is not independent validation of master-dark uncertainty. Per-ramp line detrending would remove the slope fluctuation of interest. A `STEP50` late-time segment does not establish the same first-eight read history as `SPARS50`; a longer ramp needs explicit read-time, reset/zero-read, and processing-convention closure before prefix comparison. These are future-design conditions, not measured findings.

RAW primary headers record `SAMP_SEQ`, `NSAMP`, `SUBARRAY`, `EXPSTART`, `EXPTIME`, and the source root names. The RAW `ATODGN*` and `READNSE*` fields inspected here are zero placeholders, `CAL_VER` is blank, and `DARKFILE` is `N/A`; they do not establish calibrated gain/read-noise equality or processing version. Exact per-read times, quadrant behavior, calibration reference versions, and training membership in any master dark remain unverified. No 4–8-ramp cohort is released for a covariance calculation. A separately bounded metadata pass through more candidate headers, followed by same-mode/read-time closure, would be needed first.

Machine-readable audit: `runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_hst_pixel_feasibility/dark_ramp_metadata/{candidate-header-ledger.csv,result.json,manifest.json}`. The two product-query stages and both header-selection amendments were frozen before their respective network requests. FITS byte ranges returned HTTP 206 and ended at the primary-header `END` card; no FITS data blocks were requested.

## Additive nearest-root continuation

After the first pass, a separately frozen 120-second/10-MB allowance checked 18 more date-ranked long exposures per visit, interleaving visits. This **revises the first-pass feasibility finding**: six search-visit and eight template-visit products are verified BLANK-filter `DARK`, full-frame `SPARS50`, `NSAMP=16`, with public RAW and IMA products. They have `EXPTIME=702.938171` s and `SAMPZERO=2.911756` s. The search candidates are 10.87–11.27 days before the first science visit; the template candidates are 12.14–12.94 days before the second. The science FLT headers are full-frame `SPARS50`, `NSAMP=8`, also with `SAMPZERO=2.911756` s. Exact read-time/prefix and reset/zero-read equivalence **remain unverified**; `NSAMP=16` is not silently treated as an identical eight-read realization. RAW `ATODGN*`/`READNSE*` fields are zero placeholders whereas calibrated science FLT values are populated, so they do not close gain/read-noise equality.

The candidate roots, MAST identifiers, dates, verified modes, and header hashes are in `candidate-cohort.csv`; `remaining-result.json` and `remaining-manifest.json` preserve the additive continuation. Together with the original and 352-s diagnostic stages, 66 distinct long exposures per visit now have checked headers. Thirteen search and seven template ±30-day long exposures remain uninspected because the additive pass reached 115.20 of 120 seconds. The 6/8 candidates satisfy the requested *metadata feasibility* count and were selected without examining pixels or noise. They do not release a covariance calibration: a future protocol must close first-eight timing, RAW reference-pixel/nonlinearity processing, detector gain and state, and possible master-dark training overlap before using measurements. The initial `result.json` and its 283.69-second budget remain intact as the historical first-pass record.

Six of the eight search roots and all eight template roots have already passed the header check. The two unqueried search roots are `idbx37mjq` and `idbx36mbq`; their sampling mode remains unknown. This is a proposal for a separate metadata check, not an additional acquisition under the exhausted time allowance.
