# Next experiment: independent chromatic prediction, with the gray mode explicit

**Recommendation:** finish the bounded BayeSN distance-prior sensitivity as an implementation/identification pilot, then prioritize a **same-supernova optical-to-NIR predictive test with independently constrained calibration**. The new information comes from measured NIR fluxes and external calibration observations, not from a more flexible optical nuisance model. A separate SN–transverse-BAO shape comparison can constrain the *total* gray distance/luminosity drift under its geometric assumptions, but cannot attribute that drift to dust, calibration or intrinsic evolution. Neither route is currently a corrected cosmology result.

This is a design review, not a new scan of SN outcomes. It uses the [programme map](programme-status.md), [RAISIN audit](raisin-differential.md), [SED identification](sed-identification.md), [shared calibration analysis](shared-calibration-uncertainty.md), and the [frozen BayeSN pilot](bayesn-distance-identification.md) under `runs/research_2026_09_26/bayesn_distance_identification/`. ## What is and is not identifiable

At the observational level a calibrated apparent magnitude contains an intrinsic SED/luminosity contribution, a distance modulus, attenuation and an instrument calibration term. More bands constrain combinations of these terms; they do not automatically assign physical labels.

| Mode | Measurement that can independently constrain it | What remains unmeasured by the present optical residual test |
|---|---|---|
| Observer-band zero points and passband shifts | Actual standard-star counts/magnitudes, independently calibrated stellar spectra, measured throughput; same SN observed through independently calibrated overlapping instruments | SN colors alone can trade calibration against mean SED or dust. A covariance eigenvector does not measure a signed offset. Stellar calibration errors shared with the SN model must remain shared. |
| Host reddening and extinction-law shape | Same-SN optical–NIR colors and phase evolution, with externally constrained intrinsic SED diversity and calibration; spectral line/continuum information can restrict specific intrinsic alternatives | At AV=0 the likelihood contains no RV information. A fitted color coefficient is not an extinction measurement. Galaxy attenuation or a host dust proxy does not directly determine the pencil-beam SN extinction. |
| Intrinsic chromatic diversity | Held-out spectrophotometry, line ratios/profiles and multi-phase optical–NIR data from physical objects outside model training | The broad SED family already constructed can imitate the optical calibration pattern. A tight trained SED prior restricts that freedom conditionally; it is not a new observation of each distant SN. |
| Host/environment-linked gray luminosity | Independent host distances, or same-host SN differences, with measured dust/SED and selection controlled | Same-host comparisons cancel distance and test relative luminosity within those hosts; they do not identify arbitrary mean evolution across redshift or make galaxy age equal progenitor age. |
| Arbitrary gray evolution versus distance | External distance-shape information, e.g. transverse BAO plus distance duality and a constant comoving ruler; absolute anchors constrain an intercept | Optical/NIR fluxes alone retain `mu(z)->mu(z)+g(z), M(z)->M(z)-g(z)`. Proper priors can select a point along this degeneracy but do not break it observationally. Achromatic opacity remains in the same total-drift combination. |
| Selection-induced mean shifts | A generated-to-selected likelihood using the actual measured pipeline, including nondetections/failures and calibrated population/noise support | Better residual RMS on selected objects does not establish smaller distance bias. Existing REDCOV interventions and common-classifier controls expose sensitivity without certifying the real noise law. |

The immediate main-path gap is therefore E04/E07/E11 identification, with E02/E03/E09 as mandatory controls. E10 and E12 are downstream. The already demonstrated observer/SED ambiguity and RAISIN covariance discrepancy are reasons to test specific measurement predictions, not evidence of new cosmology.

## BayeSN pilot: proper amplitude integration is necessary, but its role is limited

The SED agent's frozen protocol keeps the M20 SED, residual-SED prior and gray scatter fixed, and compares a redshift-distance prior with the same proper broad distance prior for every object. That is a useful controlled question: how much does the inferred dust-population difference depend on the external distance prior, **conditional on the trained M20 model, calibration bridge and selected sample**? It does not remove M20's training assumptions or identify unrestricted gray evolution. The [official implementation](https://github.com/bayesn/bayesn/tree/08eef9e54188f601506ef9f7f47fc65f9f52a946) and [BayeSN filter documentation](https://bayesn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/filters.html) distinguish trained models, filter definitions and reference-spectrum conventions; they should not be silently interchanged.

For fixed conditional SED f and fixed measurement covariance C, write `y ~ N(a f,C)`, `a=exp[-K(D−Dref)]`, `K=0.4 ln10`, and `D=mu+deltaM`. Set `q=fᵀC⁻¹f`, `b=fᵀC⁻¹y`, `c=yᵀC⁻¹y`. The positive-amplitude profile is at `max(0,b/q)`, with the zero boundary understood as a limit. This is a geometry diagnostic. It omits the prior measure and nuisance-volume factors needed for inference. If C changes with amplitude, this profile expression itself no longer suffices; the full covariance and log determinant enter.

With `mu~Uniform(l,u)` and `deltaM~Normal(0,sigma0²)`, the correct density is

`p(D) = [Phi((D−l)/sigma0) − Phi((D−u)/sigma0)]/(u−l)`.

The required likelihood is the one-dimensional integral of `N(y;a(D)f,C) p(D)` over D. A uniform-magnitude prior is proportional to `1/a` in amplitude coordinates, not uniform a. Its integral is therefore not the flat-amplitude Gaussian-CDF formula. An improper uniform prior in D has a divergent faint-end integral because the Gaussian flux likelihood approaches a nonzero constant as a approaches zero. The frozen finite bounds and all normalizers matter, especially at low SNR. Use a stable log-CDF difference, verify tails, and report every stated bound sensitivity rather than choosing the most convenient one.

The saved `amplitude-algebra-check.json` verifies the convolved prior normalization and compares nested versus convolved integration to about 4e−14 in a synthetic vector example. This is an algebra check, not a BayeSN operator or posterior validation. The operator still needs source-linked bandpass, zeropoint, redshift/time, sign and error conventions. In particular, the SED audit found public B_CSP reference magnitudes 9.896 versus 9.882 in the released RAISIN calibration. A 0.014-mag convention mismatch must be bridged explicitly; it must not be fitted away as dust. Transform data flux and covariance consistently if changing data units, and retain the associated model/training calibration assumptions. Both prior arms must use the same declared bridge.

No SALT model covariance should be added to a BayeSN model already carrying latent residual SED variation. Use the native measurement-noise prescription and explicit latent epsilon. The RAISIN source's NIR amplitude-only SNooPy fit is not an independent measurement of AV, RV or intrinsic shape. Current public wrappers also differ in SNR cuts, peak-time treatment and dust priors; the legacy single-SN Stan file is not the missing 2024 population-analysis executable. This pilot must remain a labelled reconstruction unless those exact execution inputs are recovered.

When broad-prior boundaries are remote, flux identifies D, not mu and deltaM separately. The gray component retains its assumed distribution; fixing sigma0=.088 mag does not measure the actual high-redshift gray scatter or mean. Report D and prior-sensitive distance decompositions separately. A posterior-width ratio is not a percentage of information independently supplied by colors.

## Additional physical-support gate from the actual dust law

The public BayeSN F99 spline, evaluated over M20's 3000–18500 Å domain, gives **negative A_lambda/AV for some permitted low RV values**. At RV=.5 its minimum is −.299924 at 7213.5 Å, with negative extinction from 6180.2 to 9367.5 Å; at RV=.6 the minimum is −.112249. For AV>0 this amplifies flux, so that part of this empirical law cannot represent a passive foreground extinction screen.

The [source-law check](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/astra_design/next_experiment_review/bayesn-dust-support.json) extracts the actual public spline functions and compares them with an independent SciPy natural spline to 1.78e−14. For this law and domain, the transition to nonnegative extinction is RV≈.6834479. This is **not a universal physical lower bound on RV**, a measurement of posterior mass, or evidence that published results were driven by that tail. Preserve the paper's RV>=.5 arm for faithful prior sensitivity; freeze a separately labelled admissibility diagnostic and record any affected posterior/prior mass before physical interpretation. Do not clamp negative extinction or silently rewrite the original prior after seeing an outcome.

## Recovery and convergence gates before an observed population claim

The frozen four-chain Rhat<=1.01, bulk ESS>=400, tail ESS>=200, zero-divergence and energy checks are appropriate minimum gates. Apply them to the derived high-minus-low dust differences and the latent degeneracy directions, not only a few convenient hyperparameters. Repeat independent seeds; require differences compatible with MC error. Check quadrature doubling and independent integration at log-likelihood error <=1e−6 and scaled gradient error <=1e−5, including negative measured flux, b<=0, AV=0, weak NIR coverage and distant amplitude boundaries. Recheck eager/JIT/NumPy likelihood and gradient agreement in the new operator before sampling.

The initial six-SN metadata-selected pilot and ten synthetic datasets are a recovery screen, not a coverage certificate. Tests must include known zero and nonzero dust, residual-SED draws, a gray offset that cannot be learned from colors, a signed calibration perturbation, and noise/selection misspecification. At AV=0, apparent recovery of a tight RV from the likelihood should fail the identification test. A finite posterior in that case reflects the prior. Nonlinear degeneracy, proper-prior tails and shared calibration must be present in a later full decision-rule calibration. Before comparing real hypotheses, require simulated false-preference and coverage performance at the scientifically relevant distance-shift scale; do not derive that scale from the most attractive real score.

The profile invariance check means rescaling the model reference f with a compensating amplitude, or transforming y, f and C together as a change of units. Scaling y alone while leaving absolute errors unchanged is not an invariant operation. Enforce the trained model's actual phase/filter support: M20 has phase knots −10 through +40 days and explicitly excludes u/U in its INFO, despite its numerical 3000 Å endpoint. These are operator gates, not reasons to select easier observed outcomes.

## Highest-information executable extension

1. **Freeze a common calibrated flux operator.** Use the actual RAISIN optical and HST/CSP NIR photometry and calibration files already inventoried. Resolve the per-band zeropoint/passband bridge on reference spectra and recorded calibration observations. Retain global calibration coordinates shared between low/high redshift and optical/NIR; do not replace them with independent per-object error bars. Include all signed measurements under declared data-quality masks. Keep the known shared-SN and training-overlap ledger.

2. **Make a genuinely held-out NIR prediction.** Within the fixed M20 family, fit optical epochs only and predict the measured NIR epochs with amplitude, dust and intrinsic SED parameters marginalized, not re-optimized on the held-out NIR. Predictive distributions must retain shared calibration and population uncertainty. Use high-redshift RAISIN objects outside M20 training as the principal external prediction set after aliases close. The current name audit identifies 29 CSP matches to M20 training; those objects are internal checks, not external validation. Low-z objects without a roster match still require alias/coordinate verification. This proposal is distinct from comparing already bias-corrected optical and NIR distance tables.

3. **Compare only prespecified competing causes.** A calibration branch changes the measured observer-band response with externally measured star/throughput priors; a dust branch changes nonnegative extinction and its explicitly normalized population; an intrinsic branch uses independently trained chromatic covariance or specified spectral modes. Hold population/training data fixed and propagate uncertainty. Forecast their difference on the actual retained NIR band/phase support before reading those predictions. If the predicted separation is smaller than conditional and shared uncertainty, stop at a power limit. Do not invoke SALT outside its supported wavelength domain to manufacture a comparison.

4. **Test transfer rather than relabel the winning branch.** Leave entire physical SNe out of training; preserve survey and redshift blocks. Follow the best predictive distinction with a new calibration/SED measurement, not a fitted correction to DES distances. The HST NIR data supply real wavelength leverage, but source selection, limited cadence, common calibration and inherited low-z training still limit physical attribution. The informative outcome can be rejection of a *specified* dust/calibration explanation without uniquely identifying the remainder.

Audit whether object or epoch inclusion depends on measured NIR flux before scoring: if it does, the target is a selection-conditioned NIR predictive distribution. Selecting NIR detections and then scoring them as an untruncated holdout is not an independent prediction test.

External spectra offer a further restriction on the intrinsic branch, but not every public spectrum is an independent continuum calibration. The [SNfactory Twins repository](https://github.com/snfactory/twins_embedding) provides estimated maximum-light spectra and analysis code; its README distinguishes those from unreleased-at-the-time preprocessing inputs. The [ZTF-SEDm release](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07696) provides useful spectral information, but its flux calibration is tied back to ZTF photometry. Treat line/shape information as additional data while retaining that continuum-calibration dependence. No new spectral outcomes were selected or fitted for this review.

## Complementary gray-shape constraint from transverse BAO

For BAO nodes inside SN redshift support, form a common-shape contrast of the SN amplitude curve with `5 log10[(1+z) DM(z)/rd]`, leaving one shared intercept free. Under distance duality and a constant ruler this measures the total relative luminosity/opacity/calibration/selection drift without selecting a LambdaCDM expansion curve. It is distinct from E05's minimum correction required by a particular expansion family. It still cannot distinguish its physical components or measure an absolute luminosity scale.

Before outcomes, freeze the SN interpolation/basis and BAO matching rule. Use the *marginal* transverse covariance submatrix, not a covariance conditioned on fitted radial distances. Respect BAO effective-redshift compression and broad window functions; a central redshift is not automatically a delta-function distance measurement. Test interpolation and compression sensitivities against independent synthetic curves at the target drift scale. Do not extrapolate to the highest BAO nodes outside the SN sample. Released SN distances also retain their existing standardization/BBC assumptions, so this would first constrain drift of that released distance product, not raw luminosity evolution. Root can pursue the inexpensive design/power calculation in parallel; the chromatic NIR test remains more directly discriminating between dust and SED alternatives.

## Minimum route to an actual bias-corrected distance likelihood

A defensible signed correction requires an independently supported calibration/extraction or population change and a controlled nominal-versus-changed processing chain. For a calibration comparison, the minimum realistic experiment uses the same raw SN measurements, source-linked nominal and independently star-constrained revised calibration, coherently retrained SEDs, refitted light-curve/population parameters, and regenerated selection/bias corrections under both branches. The present nine/twelve shared modes and stellar sensitivities are valuable controlled uncertainty directions, but do not establish the actual signed correction.

The final object is a joint likelihood for distance parameters after integrating shared calibration/training and population variables with a validated selection normalization. A BayeSN distance posterior conditioned on a cosmological distance prior is not that likelihood; it cannot simply be fed into a second cosmology fit. If posterior products are used, their prior and global coupling must be removed/accounted for exactly. Overlapping training and inference observations must enter once. Likewise, explicit nuisance coordinates replace their corresponding marginalized covariance contribution; do not append the full released distance systematic matrix again.

Require successful input/output closure, recovery of injected signed corrections, selection-aware distance coverage, held-out flux adequacy and calibrated false preference before calling the resulting distance likelihood bias-corrected. A physical interpretation may remain nonidentified even then. Publishing the supported joint luminosity–distance region and the unresolved gray freedom is a valid scientific outcome.

## Independent calibration-bridge and local-information review

The released KCOR throughputs, primary spectra and primary magnitudes define an observation operator in the released FLUXCAL convention. Supplying those as custom BayeSN filters, while leaving measured flux and errors unchanged, is a controlled way to evaluate the fixed M20 SED against that measurement convention. It is not an independently supported recalibration. The M20 mean surface, trained intrinsic covariance and their calibration history remain fixed assumptions. This custom bridge is also not proof of the exact 2024 paper's preprocessing; that execution link is still missing.

For genuinely identical passbands, `m_new=m_old+delta` implies `s=10^(-.4 delta)` and `F_new=s F_old`. A pure coordinate change must transform the predicted flux and the whole covariance with the same diagonal matrix S: `C_new=S C_old S^T`. The Gaussian density changes only by the known coordinate Jacobian `-log|S|`; physical information is invariant. If passbands differ, the response to a SN spectrum is SED-dependent. A reference-star zero-point ratio alone cannot transport the SN flux. The reported DES and HST throughput differences therefore require explicit spectral integration. They are not evidence of a calibration error in either analysis.

An [independent raw-table check](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/astra_design/next_experiment_review/bridge-check/reference-fisher-check.json) integrates the released CSP-B throughput and BD17 reference spectrum using two-node Gaussian quadrature on the merged knots. This is exact for the assumed piecewise-linear throughput and spectrum. The released primary has native same-curve magnitude 9.89611433 versus released 9.88199997, giving native-minus-release +14.11436 millimag and flux scale .98708433. Native versus released CSP-B throughput changes this result by less than 5e−11 mag under the same integration. The agent's native-grid Simpson result, +12.26453 millimag, differs by 1.85 millimag; its common-grid cubic/Simpson result differs by .1704 millimag. This is a demonstrated integration/interpolation sensitivity, not an empirically measured calibration bias. The signed transform is correct; precision claims must account for the numerical convention.

For the local information calculation, let g be the whitened gray-amplitude derivative, E the whitened shape and residual-SED derivatives in the actual trained-prior coordinates, and d the dust derivatives. With unit Normal priors on the E coordinates, first marginalizing their local linear response gives `K=I+E E^T`. Profiling a free gray amplitude then gives `I_d=d^T[K^-1-K^-1 g(g^T K^-1 g)^-1 g^T K^-1]d`. A proper external amplitude variance adds its `sigma_D^2 g g^T` to K instead. This independent data-space calculation reproduces the saved parameter-space Schur calculation to 1.92e−8 absolute information units. It is local likelihood-plus-prior curvature at the frozen latent state, not a recovered observed posterior or the full nonlinear marginal likelihood. A finite linearized covariance approximation does not automatically include parameter-dependent covariance derivatives.

The companion unconstrained-SED stress projects d off the full nuisance span `[g,E]`. Positive information after imposing the trained SED prior may coexist with negligible information in this projection. A separate [synthetic check](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/astra_design/next_experiment_review/bridge-check/algebra.json) demonstrates exactly this: trained-prior information eigenvalues .915 and .960 become zero to 2.1e−30 when the same SED directions are free. This is not a recommendation to make the intrinsic SED arbitrarily flexible; it identifies which restriction supplies the apparent dust distinction. Fixed endpoint constraints, phase interpolation, peak time and trained shape priors are also model assumptions. At AV=0 the RV derivative must vanish; distance modulus and a free intrinsic gray magnitude have identical flux derivatives. Neither degeneracy is removed by a successful sampler.

## Separate signed-flux integrity gate

Root and Sol are auditing the unusually all-positive released pilot fluxes and possible source/export transformations. This review does not establish the cause and does not repeat their row comparison. Positive values alone do not distinguish absolute-value folding, selection/truncation, censoring, magnitude-to-flux transformation, source fitting constraints, or a legitimate finite realization. These mechanisms imply different likelihoods.

For latent independent signed flux `x~Normal(f,sigma^2)`, the three simple cases are:

* If the export is `r=abs(x)`, the density at r>0 is `Normal(r;f,sigma^2)+Normal(-r;f,sigma^2)`. Do not guess a sign per row from a preferred SN model.
* If only positive x are sampled and the likelihood is conditional on that selection, the density is `Normal(r;f,sigma^2)/Phi(f/sigma)`. The denominator depends on the model and cannot be dropped.
* If the original cadence is known and nonpositive values were replaced by a missing indicator, each retained signed value supplies its Gaussian density and each known missing epoch supplies `Phi(-f/sigma)`. Unknown missing cadence cannot be invented. Other reasons for missingness require their own model.

At f=0, folded and positive-truncated retained values both have mean `.79788456 sigma`, yet their local information differs. The [independent normalization/score check](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/astra_design/next_experiment_review/bridge-check/sign-likelihood-algebra.json) gives information per datum of 0 for folding, `(1-2/pi)/sigma^2` for conditional truncation, and `(1/2+1/pi)/sigma^2` per attempted epoch for known censoring. Full signed Gaussian measurements give `1/sigma^2`. Folding is locally nonregular at zero and loses the sign of f; boundary recovery cannot be assessed by a naive Gaussian Fisher approximation. A plain Gaussian fit to positive retained values would instead mistake their selection/folding mean for signal. With cross-epoch covariance, the likelihood involves sign sums or multivariate selection integrals; multiplying these univariate formulas is justified only under independent measurement noise.

Before any observed recovery, establish the transformation and original epoch/uncertainty provenance from source and matched records. Then freeze a synthetic test with the actual fixed cadence and quoted error scales, signed latent Gaussian draws, known amplitudes including zero/low-SNR tails, and precisely the source-established export/selection rule. Compare the correct generative likelihood with the naive released-positive Gaussian likelihood using proper amplitude priors, retain failed and boundary cases, and test coverage and signed distance bias across independent seeds. The mechanism and thresholds must be chosen from source evidence, not from whichever likelihood best fits the real fluxes. If the source cannot resolve the transformation, retain explicitly separate likelihood scenarios and stop short of a repaired distance likelihood. Neither a nearest-date source match nor an inferred model sign establishes exact sign repair.
