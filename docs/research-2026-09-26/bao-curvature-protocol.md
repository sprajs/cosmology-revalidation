# Curvature-independent extension of the exploratory BGS inequality

This is a post-result extension, not an untouched primary test. Retain the
same13 compressed DESI DR2 observations and constant comoving ruler, positive
expansion and homogeneous/isotropic metric assumptions. Permit flat, open and
closed spatial geometry with no external curvature prior. In closed geometry
retain positive angular distances on the first-antipode branch through the
highest measured transverse redshift. Do not fit a dark-energy equation of
state, matter density, H0 or the ruler's physical length.

Let b=.295, t_b=ln(1+b), a=b/(1+b), B=DV(b)/rd, and
g_j=(1+z_j)DH(z_j)/rd for each of the six later radial measurements. Let D
be the transverse DM/rd at the highest measured transverse redshift (2.33).
If q>=0 from0 to z_j, g is nonincreasing there, so DC_b/rd>=t_b*g_j and
DH_b/rd>=g_j/(1+b). Hence DM_b/rd<=sqrt[B^3/(a*g_j)].

For closed radius R in rd units, DM=R sin(DC/R) and R>=D. If
D>sqrt[B^3/(a*g_j)], the BGS point is before transverse-distance turnover
on the first-antipode branch. Provided t_b*g_j/D<pi/2, monotonicity in both
DC and R gives DM_b/rd>=D sin(t_b*g_j/D). Thus the necessary condition is

    B >= {a*g_j*[D sin(t_b*g_j/D)]^2}^(1/3).

Flat/open geometry has a stronger lower bound and therefore also obeys this
necessary condition. Verify these inequalities on analytically integrated
random piecewise-nonnegative-q histories, including after-turnover high-z
distances that still pass the branch gate. Check the flat-radius limit and
independent numerical integrals. A failed branch gate is an inconclusive
certificate, never grounds to force the inequality.

Use one-sided simultaneous Gaussian marginal confidence bounds, which require
no assumed cross-correlation: B_upper=Bhat+k*sigma_B,
g_lower=ghat-k*sigma_g, D_lower=Dhat-k*sigma_D. Evaluate the branch/domain gates
at those conservative limits, and the inequality using the monotone lower
g,D and upper B. Find the largest k with a violated necessary condition.
For one chosen radial measurement, the false-certificate probability is at
most3*Phi(-k). For the full six-radial family with shared B,D bounds, it is at
most8*Phi(-k), capped at1. Report every radial result and the simultaneous
bound, without claiming this correction erases earlier exploration. No Wilks
conversion, best-curvature posterior, or calibrated bias estimate is implied.

This tests whether a flatness assumption alone is essential to the earlier
conditional past-acceleration indication. Ruler evolution, mean extraction
biases, extra variance, redshift-kernel compression, anisotropy and multiple
antipodes remain distinct assumptions. The result cannot localize q(0).
