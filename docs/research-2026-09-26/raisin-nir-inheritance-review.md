# RAISIN NIR timing inheritance: source and ten-object audit

The released DES NIR-only distance branch inherits a fixed peak date from each released photometry header. It does **not** import the final released optical or optical+NIR fitted peak, stretch, or AV per object. The exact earlier fit or processing command that wrote the header peak is still unidentified. This resolves the immediate NIR fitting link while preserving the upstream ambiguity noted in [raisin-differential.md](raisin-differential.md).

The [frozen review plan](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_nir_inheritance_review/review-plan.json), [read-only checker](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_nir_inheritance_review/check.py), [ten-object ledger](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_nir_inheritance_review/des16-peak-inheritance.csv), and [result with source hashes](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_nir_inheritance_review/result.json) preserve every join. Exact author blobs for `data/raisin_t0.txt`, raw NIR FITRES, and a raw combined FITRES were acquired at the pinned author commit and verified against Git-blob hashes ([acquisition](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_nir_inheritance_review/author-acquisition.json)). The raw NIR FITRES header says **SNANA v11_03c**, so I separately acquired its tagged `snana.car` and `snlc_fit.car` by commit/blob hash ([source manifest](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_nir_inheritance_review/source-v11_03c/acquisition.json)). The tag and table header match is source provenance, not proof of the original compiled binary or environment.

In the v11_03c source, `snana.car:4273–4274` reads photometry `PEAKMJD` into single-precision `SNLC_SEARCH_PEAKMJD` (type at `:811–882`). `OPT_SETPKMJD` defaults to zero (`:5844`), and a new estimate runs only if positive (`:17598–17599`); the released DES NIR NML does not override it. `snlc_fit.car:8050–8065` copies that value into the initial peak and uses `INISTP_PEAKMJD`. The [released NIR NML](../../sources/repos/djones1040__RAISIN_DataRelease@a383c4b/lcfitting/REFAC_DES_RAISIN_nir_sys.nml) sets `INISTP_PEAKMJD=0`, `INIVAL_SHAPE=1`, `INISTP_SHAPE=0`, `INIVAL_AV=0`, `INISTP_AV=0`, and `FILTLIST_FIT='JH'` (lines 90–105). The SNooPy shape/AV assignment in `snlc_fit.car:8132–8138` and fit-filter parser at `:7318–7340` support that reading. The one fitted light-curve parameter is distance/amplitude; J/H flux and quoted errors supply its direct epochs. The NIR rest-phase window is −15 to +45 days, with the v11_03c first-iteration phase rejection at `snlc_fit.car:4694–4715`. A changed inherited peak can therefore change NIR model phase and accepted J/H rows, even though peak is fixed during that NIR fit.

All ten DES16 photometry files have **two identical** `PEAKMJD` header lines. For all ten, the raw author NIR `PKMJDINI` and `PKMJD` equal the header after the source's single-precision conversion and four-decimal FITRES printing; `PKMJDERR=0`, stretch=1 with zero error, and AV=0 with zero error. The processed released NIR peak equals the author's raw NIR peak in all ten. The apparent ≤0.0016-day differences from one-decimal header text are exactly the single-precision representation, not a distinct fit. All ten exact CID joins and fixed-parameter checks passed.

The author [SIMLIB generator](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_sign_source/raisin_cosmo/genSimlib.py) reads a **different** `data/raisin_t0.txt` array (lines 21–24) and writes the DES SIMLIB `PEAKMJD` rounded to one decimal (lines 190–209). The exact pinned t0 file closes to all ten SIMLIB peak entries; none equals the released NIR photometry header peak. Its upstream estimator is not established. The available DES simulation input sets `USE_SIMLIB_PEAKMJD:1`, generates grizJH, and applies optical griz search/quality cuts ([input](../../runs/research_2026_09_26/raisin_sign_source/sim/inputs/DES/sim_DES_SNOOPY.input), lines 9–13, 38, 62–75). These are available source settings, not a certified full published bias-correction execution. The [cadence audit](raisin-simulation-sign-accounting.md) separately shows that the SIMLIB inherits the positive-only released optical schedule.

At **fixed photometry header, J/H observations, redshift and fit settings**, restoring signed optical rows does not provide new direct griz likelihood terms to the released NIR-only fit: J/H alone are in `FILTLIST_FIT`, and shape/AV/peak remain fixed. The release's DES g/r/i/z zero-point FITOPT019–022 outputs confirm this static separation for the ten-object cohort: their NIR peak, shape, AV, raw DLMAG, exported bias correction, residual and mass correction are identical to FITOPT000 at printed values. This is a check on those *released static variants*, not a test of restored signed rows.

If signed optical data were used to **re-estimate the upstream header peak**, the NIR amplitude and phase mask could change through that peak. The NIR branch does not import the optical fitted stretch or AV: those remain fixed at 1 and 0. The released optical and optical+NIR NMLs instead fit griz and grizJH with free peak, shape and AV, so optical rows enter those separate fits directly. Optical discovery/follow-up and the available simulation's griz cuts can also alter which objects enter the sample; the published bias corrections are fixed exported products, not quantities that update when an individual header or optical row changes. A consistent selection/bias rerun would need the upstream peak-generation procedure, the full attempt/selection record and the effective simulation/bias-correction execution and inputs. None of these is closed by the released NIR FITRES or the available `sim.py` (`bias()` is a stub). Shared redshift, host mass, NIR photometry/calibration and empirical SNooPy population assumptions remain separate information paths. No correction magnitude, preferred fit, or cosmology result follows from this audit.
