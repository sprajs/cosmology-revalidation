## Cosmic rays, hot pixels and timing are part of the estimand

A RAW cosmic-ray step is a genuine contribution to total observed second moments. Its step has nonzero projection onto h, so it can dominate the scalar statistic. Stable hot-pixel mean ramps may cancel between repeats, but their shot noise and unstable dark current do not. Digitizer saturation may hide latent variation. Retain such contributions, original flags and digital-endpoint counts; do not clip, winsorize, remove a high-second-difference epoch, or declare a lower-tail estimate to be electronic noise. Reference-masked and geometry-only results describe their respective digitized measurement populations.

Even without those effects, E[δyδyᵀ/2] is K only for independent equal-covariance repeats with equal means. Otherwise it includes the average covariance, an unequal-mean outer product and inter-ramp covariance terms. Preserve individual pairs and detector blocks. Three/four pairs do not become many independent temporal realizations through millions of pixels. Do not report a Gaussian significance or quantitative ERR rescaling.

The timing sensitivity is algebraic until a physically justified error bound exists. A shared time offset cancels from h; a uniform scale t′=(1+ε)t rescales h by1/(1+ε) and the second moment by1/(1+ε)². Read-specific time errors can mix a true mean ramp into the measured slope and require their own covariance/bounds. Printed floating precision does not bound the TDF condition. The NORMAL search sensitivity checks how the result changes on a metadata-defined pair; a difference from other pairs cannot causally identify TDF because epoch/history also differ.
