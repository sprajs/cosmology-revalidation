# Independent challenge: slope variance and the CDS convention

The source algebra is correct for its declared independent-read comparison. It is not enough to establish that actual FLT errors are too large. The distinction is observationally testable with repeated ramp data, without any supernova distance model.

The pinned CALWF3 3.7.3 `cridcalc.c` sets `rdns=21/gain`, labels it CDS, constructs the fixed-branch linear slope from endpoint-weighted samples, uses `S/denom` for its read-variance term, and adds the realized endpoint source-plus-dark increment divided by duration squared. The final-read input ERR also chooses the weight-power branch. A successful, unsplit interval is therefore different from a generic independent Gaussian weighted least-squares fit; rejected reads, interval splitting, branch selection and the separate one/two-sample routes remain additional conditions. None of those branches was inferred from a fitted astronomical distance here.

The [official WFC3 Instrument Handbook §5.7.3](https://hst-docs.stsci.edu/wfc3ihb/chapter-5-wfc3-detector-characteristics-and-performance/5-7-ir-detector-characteristics-and-performance) defines CDS noise through a difference between reads and describes the approximately21-electron measurement as coming from short RAPID ramps. That convention fixes a difference variance at the calibration cadence. It does not by itself give the covariance matrix of SPARS50 reads or prove that individual reads are independent.

For accepted times t and fixed weights u, let h_i=u_i(t_i−tbar)/Σu(t−tbar)². Then hᵀ1=0 and hᵀt=1. For arbitrary read covariance K, the read contribution is hᵀKh. If a shared zeroth read is subtracted, A=I−1e₀ᵀ and hᵀA=hᵀ: common-reference subtraction does not itself double the slope variance. This identity holds for correlated reads as well as independent ones, provided the slope coefficients are held fixed and an intercept is fitted. It is not permission to assume K is diagonal. Common additive offsets cancel; temporally varying drifts need not. Reference-pixel corrections can also couple time samples and detector positions.

Only with independent equal-variance reads does σsingle²=σCDS²/2 follow. The independent calculation in `check.py` reproduces reported/actual read-variance ratios2.000–2.204 for the declared seven equally spaced reads and six powers. It also checks cumulative-Poisson covariance `r min(t_i,t_j)` against independent increments. These are conditional algebra checks, not observed error-scale estimates.

A concrete nonidentification counterexample is saved: stationary AR(1) covariance families can all have adjacent difference variance441 electrons² while producing very different slope variances. For equal weights, ρ=0 yields a reported/actual ratio2, ρ=.25 gives1.154 and ρ=.5 gives.648. These are illustrative mathematical alternatives, not fitted detector models, and the true calibration/test lag difference adds another unresolved issue. Thus even the direction of a correction does not follow from the CDS label alone.

The source weights and reported Poisson term depend on the realized ramp. Conditioning on the chosen power does not preserve an unconditional Gaussian noise distribution. Rejection and segmentation are additional selection operations. The fixed-power coefficient table should not be advertised as an exact pivot for observed ERR or used to rescale the frozen aperture results. Likewise, agreement with a roughly.7 repeat-variance ratio would not identify read noise as its cause.

## What next measures the missing quantity

Do not estimate the needed covariance by fitting and subtracting an intercept plus slope from each individual IMA ramp. That observes only RKRᵀ, where R annihilates both1 and t; it removes exactly the random-slope direction of interest. `check.py` demonstrates that adding αttᵀ changes slope variance byα while leaving this detrended covariance unchanged to numerical precision.

The strongest small next control is repeated dark ramps with the same SPARS50/NSAMP8 cadence and matching processing, taken close enough in time to constrain detector-state changes. Use cross-ramp differences or a mean dark pattern estimated from other ramps, not each ramp's fitted slope, so random slope fluctuations survive. With two independent matched ramps and stable underlying dark signal, Cov(y_A−y_B)/2 estimates the within-ramp covariance including its slope direction. With4–8 ramps, retain pair identity, detector quadrant and spatial blocks; two different dark states are not automatically independent draws from one noise law. Photon dark current remains present and must be modelled or reported as part of the total dark-ramp noise. Cold reference pixels separately constrain electronic components but are not automatically equivalent to photosensitive pixels.

Required inputs are the actual RAW or suitable IMA read sequence, each sample's TIME and DQ/ERR, the zeroth/reference-read handling, contemporaneous dark/nonlinearity/flat references, and the exact read-selection/weighting route. Preserve signed values; masks should be predeclared from calibration quality or separate exposures, with no residual clipping. DQ-dependent eligibility is a conditional measurement policy, not proof of missing-at-random noise. A source rerun or transparent source-matched implementation should reproduce saved FLT slope/ERR before assigning a component difference to CALWF3.

Estimate covariance across time **and neighboring detector pixels**, then contract it with the same slope coefficients and frozen native aperture/annulus weights. A per-pixel variance alone does not predict aperture variance. Eight ramps provide limited independent temporal replication regardless of the number of pixels; block uncertainty and detector-state limitations remain explicit. This tests a measurement-noise component. It neither reconstructs the private RAISIN photometry nor supplies a cosmology correction.
