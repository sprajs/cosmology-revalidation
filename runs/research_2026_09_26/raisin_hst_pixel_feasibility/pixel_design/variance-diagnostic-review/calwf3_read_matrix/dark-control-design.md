# Proposed bounded dark-ramp control — metadata stage only

Purpose: measure the slope-sensitive temporal and spatial noise of repeated detector ramps, independently of SN luminosities. The current source audit motivates this control; it is a follow-up investigation, not a blind physical discovery test. Sol owns the public metadata query and will not duplicate requests here. No images, ramps or observed dark residuals have been read for this design.

## Cohort feasibility before pixels

Seek4–8 full-frame WFC3/IR DARK ramps with SPARS50 and preferably NSAMP8, near the two existing science/template visits. Select within each comparable group by absolute epoch separation, then chronological EXPSTART/rootname; retain this deterministic rule before looking at pixels. Record proposal, visit, reset/sampling sequence, exposure duration, detector layout, processing version/reference files and RAW/IMA/FLT associations. Nearby dates alone do not prove equal detector state. Do not combine distinct groups to manufacture four nominal repetitions.

NSAMP>8 is an explicitly secondary feasibility route, not an exact NSAMP8 match. Its first8 reads must have the same reset convention and actual TIME sampling; any DQ/cosmic-ray classification informed by later reads must be retained as a difference from a true8-read exposure. Header SAMP8 does not by itself identify the native selected interval or weight branch. Each candidate needs product URI and size before deciding a bounded download. Missing optional header metadata are unknowns, not reasons for flux-dependent replacement.

Minimum useful inputs are four comparable repeats and recoverable read sequences, ideally RAW including zeroth/reference pixels, together with per-read times, quality and matching calibration references. With fewer repeats, this is a limited engineering case, not a covariance calibration. Sol's separate20MB metadata/300s cap remains unchanged. There is no authorization in this note to acquire ramp arrays or execute pixel analysis.

## Measurement that preserves the missing information

For matched exposure pair A/B, use same-detector-pixel read vectors in a common electron convention. After an explicitly identical reference/zero-read and nonlinearity treatment, Δy=y_A−y_B cancels a stable mean ramp without fitting a line to either member. Under independent, equally distributed ramps with identical means, E[ΔyΔyᵀ]/2 is the within-ramp covariance. Unequal means add a positive outer-product term; unequal covariance gives their average. Report those assumptions rather than assuming equal dark rates, persistence histories or amplifier state.

Calibrator darks may have contributed to the master DARKFILE used in their current IMA/FLT products. Such processed residuals are not independent validation of that reference. A common static master cancels algebraically from a truly identical linear pair, but reference creation, different processing, nonlinear corrections or sample rejection can destroy that simplification. Prefer RAW pairs for the primary component measurement, with source-defined common early processing, or establish training membership and explicitly retain its dependence. Do not fit away an exposure's own slope to remove dark current: that removes the target noise mode. Reference pixels provide a separate electronics check; they are not a substitute for photosensitive-pixel covariance.

A frozen fixed-weight slope h with hᵀ1=0 and hᵀt=1 gives direct contrast hᵀΔy. Estimate its second moment and temporal/nearby-pixel cross-products, retaining detector coordinates, quadrant and fixed spatial blocks. Use the six previously declared weight powers as labelled source-operator comparisons, not choose the one that best matches the science ratio. The actual CALWF3 estimator has data-dependent power/rejection/interval branches. Its final slope and ERR require their own exact source/product replay and unstudentized repeated-ramp comparison; a fixed-h covariance calculation cannot silently certify the adaptive estimator.

At the aperture level the target is a contraction over both pixels and reads, Σ_{p,q,i,j} w_p h_{p,i} K_{p,i;q,j} w_q h_{q,j}. Pixelwise variance estimates omit the spatial covariance required by the signed aperture/annulus statistic. Dark images have no useful astronomical WCS/PHOTFLAM for this purpose: use a predeclared detector-coordinate operator and an explicit gain/flat/PAM unit map if translating to the existing F160W aperture operator. Do not assign a sky flux to a dark exposure or mix pre-flat electrons with post-flat count-rate ERR. An unapplied common multiplicative flat requires an explicit linear transformation of both vectors and covariance.

Use disjoint chronological pairs for the small primary replication ledger. Retain signed measurements and quality flags with no |residual| clipping, no empirical error rescaling and no positive-flux selection. Spatial replication can constrain local covariance but does not turn four ramps into thousands of independent temporal realizations. Report pair and spatial-block variation separately. Dark photon noise remains part of the measured total; separating it from read noise needs a source-defined photon model or independent dark-rate information, not the assumption that a dark is noiseless.

## What this can decide

A valid repeat-dark contraction can test whether the source's independent-read/Poisson approximation describes matched detector data. It can distinguish temporal noise directions that per-ramp detrending hides and show whether spatial covariance materially changes an aperture variance. It does not by itself identify the cause of the science/template repeat deficit, because sky photons, persistence, source structure, masked apertures and calibration dependence differ. No SN distance or cosmology response follows until the measurement operator and its uncertainty are separately validated.

Metadata feasibility review is pending Sol's owned query. The executable cohort, pair list, pixel masks, exact read/calibration operators, resource budget and analysis gates must be frozen after that metadata review and before any new pixel outcomes.
