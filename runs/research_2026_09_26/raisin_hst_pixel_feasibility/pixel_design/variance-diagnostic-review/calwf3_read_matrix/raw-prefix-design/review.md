# Minimal RAW-prefix covariance experiment

The metadata now support a matched sampling experiment: in each of the two acquired dark/science pairs, the dark's first8 chronological groups and the science's8 groups have identical recorded SAMPNUM, SAMPTIME, DELTATIM, SAMP/TIME constants and1024² geometry. These are native SPARS50 prefixes, not interpolated STEP samples. The candidate pool contains6 and8 darks, but the remaining12 prefixes still need the same metadata gate before a repeated-ramp analysis. This review reads saved metadata and pinned source only; it does not read arrays, score pixels, download data or build software.

**Recommendation:** begin with a separately named raw-DN, fixed-slope pair-covariance measurement. In parallel prepare one exact native science RAW→FLT replay before claiming anything about CALWF3's reported science variance. The raw experiment has an interpretable narrower result even if full pipeline replay is not ready. A hand-written gain conversion is not an exact calibration bridge.

## 1. Smallest valid component measurement

Keep the recorded first8 groups (zero plus7 nonzero reads), ordered by SAMPNUM/time rather than EXTVER. The acquired darks use EXTVER16…9 for these reads, while the science files use8…1. Never choose the first8 extensions on disk. FITS SCI are unsigned count values encoded by BITPIX16/BZERO32768; decode in a wide signed type before subtraction. Constant NAXIS0 SAMP/TIME/ERR/DQ extensions must be expanded according to PIXVALUE. Missing image arrays are not missing measurements or arrays of zero. The template science's full DQ arrays explain its larger RAW size; file size cannot define its read sequence.

Use all predeclared candidate darks that meet metadata integrity, pairing them chronologically within visit:6 darks give3 disjoint pairs,8 give4. Source quality flags, including the acquired template dark's EXPFLAG=INDETERMINATE, are recorded and stratified; do not replace it after inspecting noise. Missing/incomplete read metadata or physically invalid exposure status stops that pair's intended interpretation, not an outcome-based search for a cleaner substitute. This is a design proposal; a separate execution freeze must resolve quality handling and exact pixel masks before array reads.

For each matched same-detector pixel p and pair k, Δy_kp is the signed difference of the two7-read vectors. A fixed ordinary-least-squares slope h, computed from the actual seven times and an intercept, satisfies hᵀ1=0 and hᵀt=1. Its units are seconds⁻¹, so hᵀΔy is DN/s. The primary second-moment contraction is (hᵀΔy)²/2. Also retain the full7×7 ΔyΔyᵀ/2 by fixed detector regions and a prescribed spatial aperture/annulus contraction of hᵀΔy. Predeclare the existing six CALWF3 weight powers only as labelled operator sensitivities; do not choose a power from agreement with the science variance ratio. No temporal line is subtracted from either ramp.

Under independent equal-covariance ramps with equal mean vectors, E[ΔyΔyᵀ/2]=K. With unequal covariances it is their average; unequal means add δμδμᵀ/2, and inter-ramp covariance subtracts its symmetric part. Thus the statistic is a measured paired second moment unless those assumptions are supported. Preserve per-pair signed mean/slope maps and spatial/quadrant summaries so changing dark current, persistence or electronics are visible. Do not estimate and remove each ramp's own slope to manufacture equality of means: that deletes the exact covariance direction at issue.

Use fixed detector-coordinate masks from metadata/reference quality, not |Δy| cuts or fitted residuals. Where quality is sample-dependent, explicitly state the conditioning and retain counts. Preserve reference pixels separately from photosensitive pixels. Spatial blocks estimate local spatial variation;3 or4 pairs per visit remain3 or4 temporal replicates. Do not pool visits as identical to inflate precision. The four-product acquisition pilot has only one dark per visit and cannot itself provide this repeat statistic.

The aperture/annulus comparison must contract the actual spatial covariance, not sum pixel variances. Freeze a native-detector geometric operator before pixels, or transport the already frozen science operator with the explicit1024→1014 trim/index and gain/flat/PAM mapping. A raw-DN geometric zero-sum contrast is useful but is not automatically the previously measured calibrated F160W aperture statistic. Do not silently equate the two.

## 2. Early correction is a separate measured operator

Pinned CALWF3 3.7.3 `doir.c:205–318` orders DQI → ZSIG → BLEVCORR → ZOFFCORR → NOISCALC → NLINCORR → DARKCORR → UNITCORR → CR rejection/slope → FLATCORR/gain. This order matters.

- `blevcorr.c:108–194` obtains a3σ-resistant mean from reference-pixel strips selected by OSCNTAB geometry, then subtracts the same scalar from the whole read. It does not update ERR. Its estimate is noisy and selected from the read, so it can induce/change cross-pixel covariance. Reproduce the source's `resistmean` and bounds exactly before calling an implementation BLEVCORR. A fixed zero-sum spatial contrast algebraically removes a uniform read offset, but post-flat/PAM weights and nonuniform gain need not sum to zero in the raw convention.
- `zoffcorr.c:71–94` subtracts the saved zero SCI, ORs DQ and subtracts the zero time; ERR/SAMP are unchanged. At the purely linear, fixed-h stage, a common zero-read vector cancels because hᵀ1=0, irrespective of its temporal correlation. That does not prove cancellation after nonlinear corrections, rejection or adaptive weights.
- `zsigcorr.c:163–235` uses the NLINFILE superzero and error, thresholds its estimated signal, sets flags and modifies the zero read. This is not simply subtracting raw read0. `doir.c:212–226` saves the original zero before ZSIG and separately bias-corrects it.
- `noiscalc.c:109–176` initializes previously empty ERR from `sqrt(readnoise²+abs(signal*gain))/gain`. This is a source prescription from data and CCDTAB, not an independently measured variance. Zero gain/readnoise cards in RAW are placeholders: `doir.c:127–148` loads CCDTAB and replaces them.
- `nlincorr.c:176–219` adds inferred zero signal before the nonlinear polynomial, subtracts it afterward, propagates flags, and handles saturation. Its current loop changes SCI without derivative propagation into ERR. Raw dark fluctuations cannot be mapped to illuminated science merely by multiplying their covariance by a scalar gain.
- `darkcorr.c:118–120` subtracts the time-matched reference and combines its ERR/DQ; `refdata.c:321–424` requires matching SAMP_SEQ/SUBTYPE and matches standard-sequence reference times within.01s. A common deterministic reference cancels from an identical linear pair, including if those darks trained it. Nevertheless self-use and the quoted reference uncertainty matter for uncertainty validation and any adaptive/nonlinear processing; master-dark independence cannot be assumed.
- `unitcorr.c:87–108` converts by sample TIME, with separate zero-read handling. `flatcorr.c:108–148` later divides by the combined flat and multiplies by the mean gain, including ERR. The acquired dark products deliberately omit ZSIGCORR, DARKCORR and FLATCORR while both acquired science inputs request all three. In particular, sharing NLINFILE does not imply sharing the nonlinear correction operator when ZSIGCORR differs. They are not science-calibrated electron/s images.

The lowest-risk next transformed measurement is exact per-read BLEVCORR followed by the same fixed raw-DN slope, with the original uncorrected contrast retained. This identifies the effect of the reference projection itself. It does not need a master-dark subtraction or a guessed photon component, and it should not be presented as a complete CALWF3 replay. Dark shot noise is still present. A photon/read decomposition needs a separate model or independent measurement; “dark” does not mean zero photon variance.

## 3. Route to the actual calibrated-prefix estimator

For a quantitative comparison with science FLT ERR, prefer an isolated pinned native HSTCAL build (or verified identical local executable) with the exact header/reference inputs. First reproduce the acquired full NSAMP8 science RAW into the already available current FLT. Check all SCI/ERR/DQ/SAMP/TIME arrays, trimming/unit conventions and active references; diagnose any mismatch before using the executable as the science operator. Record version/toolchain/reference hashes and numerical tolerance before replay, with no residual-based tolerance choice. This is current2026 calibration closure, not a reconstruction of historical RAISIN photometry.

Then make an immutable prefix input derived from each longer RAW: retain groups with SAMPNUM0…7, preserve bytes/values and raw DQ, renumber group EXTVER8…1 consistently and update only the source-required group/count/time bookkeeping with an explicit transformation ledger. Do not guess the needed header edits: derive them from the reader and native checks. Prefix extraction precedes CR rejection. The source's first-read rejection, saturation, CRREJTAB and data-dependent weighting all operate on the prefix alone. Freeze the reference choice rather than letting an online service silently choose a different context after the fact.

A native dark-prefix output using original dark switches remains a dark-pipeline diagnostic. A separate science-reference transformation is needed to contract its detector covariance into the F160W measurement convention. State each switch/reference change explicitly. Do not claim that adding a science DARKFILE makes a calibration exposure statistically identical to an illuminated observation. The same detector read component may be testable while source/sky Poisson, persistence and adaptive-weight transport remain unidentifiable from darks alone.

Downloading an IMA is **optional validation**, not logically necessary if exact native replay closes. It can be useful to check intermediate calibrated SCI values. `cridcalc.c:73–83` says CR rejection changes per-read DQ but not per-read SCI; hence a full16 IMA's early SCI could equal a prefix's pre-rejection SCI if all earlier/reference operations are shown prefix-local. This does not license its later-read-informed DQ, SAMP or fitted slope. Slicing a full-ramp FLT is impossible, and slicing IMA without proving those semantics is not exact prefix replay. Avoid a large IMA download solely to bypass these source checks.

## Quality qualification and uncertainty map

Recorded complete groups and matched times pass a structural gate; they do not resolve the template dark's INDETERMINATE exposure flag. The parent is tracing the instrument-specific quality meaning and support telemetry. Until resolved, that exposure may remain in an explicitly exploratory raw-DN ledger with its flag prominent, but must not be certified as a pipeline-valid independent calibrator. Freeze the quality-conditioned cohort before covariance outcomes; if fewer than the declared comparable repeats remain, retain an engineering result rather than quietly weakening the repeat requirement. Do not turn an unknown flag into a claim of damaged pixel values.

For reference correction b_i at read i, Cov(y_pi−b_i,y_qj−b_j)=K_pi,qj−Cov(y_pi,b_j)−Cov(b_i,y_qj)+Cov(b_i,b_j). The last three terms are precisely why adding a scalar variance estimate to each pixel is not an adequate aperture covariance model. The resistant estimate is nonlinear in reference pixels, so repeat measurements can test its final contraction without pretending its clipping mask was fixed. Keep the uncorrected and source-corrected contractions together.

A common master-dark subtraction also does not make quoted variances automatically independent. For any common deterministic calibration field D, (y_A−D)−(y_B−D)=y_A−y_B algebraically even if D was built from these exposures. But a per-exposure variance ledger may still contain D's uncertainty in both terms, and data-dependent weights/DQ can fail to commute with subtraction. This is a candidate source of shared measurement covariance to calculate, not a license to remove master uncertainty or attribute the observed science ratio before replay.
