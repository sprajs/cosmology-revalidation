# Supernova populations and cosmic acceleration: independent investigation

Scientific record frozen against sources retrieved on 20 September 2026. This report supersedes the acquisition-only interpretation of the original dossier. Numerical results are from local calculations unless explicitly labelled as an author's result. The [reproducibility guide](reproducibility.md), [experiment register](experiment-register.md), [claim ledger](claim-evidence-ledger.md), and [independent falsification audit](investigations/independent-audit.md) provide the inspectable record.

## Scientific assessment

**Host populations, dust, calibration and selection materially affect supernova inference. The examined evidence does not establish a unique extra age correction, or a robust conclusion that the universe is decelerating today.** Standard released distances favour acceleration in the cosmological and kinematic models tested here. A sufficiently large, specified luminosity-evolution correction changes that conclusion. Its numerical effect is reproducible; identifying its physical amplitude, population transport and uncertainty is the unresolved problem.

Neither extreme survives scrutiny. Existing standardization cannot be declared complete: actual dust/calibration corrections have been revised, and separate ZTF photometry retains an environmental magnitude dependence under flexible dust modelling. Conversely, historical host-age slopes cannot automatically be applied again to distances already corrected through host-dependent dust, population and selection simulations. On the same modern matched sample the additional age relation is substantially weaker, though its uncertainty does not exclude every cosmologically important effect.

The strongest new information in this investigation is not another preferred fit. It is the controlled separation of these claims: exact overlap/cut reconstruction; a correction and covariance audit; a mathematical test of the published dilution mock; counterexamples to causal identification and universal slope transport; independent distance/mapping calculations; and explicit sensitivity of acceleration to correction moments and shared slope uncertainty. These are tests on reused observations or disclosed synthetic populations, not new independent observations.

## What the observations and likelihoods are

The underlying measurements are calibrated fluxes and redshifts, with host photometry/spectroscopy providing further constraints. Light-curve shape and colour, stellar age, metallicity, dust parameters, corrected distances and cosmological parameters are successively inferred quantities. Host stellar age is not progenitor delay time; intrinsic SN colour is not reddening; galaxy attenuation is not sightline extinction. The [causal model](causal-model.md) makes the latent variables and selection paths explicit, and the [correction ledger](correction-ledger.md) records which channels are already present in each distance product.

Our main cosmological calculation begins at **released corrected magnitudes and correlated covariances**, plus compressed DESI BAO measurements. The age work begins at released inferred-age summaries and distance tables. Dovekie comparisons use release-level distances and matrices. Public author host simulations are inspected as simulations. Original CMB chains are references, not a rerun from maps. We inspected newly acquired ZTF photometry and release schemas, but did not perform an end-to-end photometry/calibration/selection reconstruction. A new raw-photometry fit without the authors' exact configuration would not be an exact reproduction of their analysis.

Pantheon+ contributes 1,590 light-curve rows representing 1,473 unique CIDs after zHD>0.01. Repeated observations keep their covariance. The matched age sample has 196 unique SDSS SNe; G11 and R19 share 33 explosions, 30 of them in this match. Papers reusing these objects, DES reanalyses and calibration/model training sets must not be counted as independent confirmations. The [current source map](literature-and-data-status.md) separates those dependencies.

## Expansion, acceleration and dark energy are different questions

For a homogeneous isotropic background, `q(z) = -1 + (1+z) H'(z)/H(z)`. In spatially flat geometry with the usual photon propagation/distance-duality assumptions, `dL=(1+z)c integral(dz/H)`. Curvature changes the distance relation; extracting q requires derivatives of an integrated observable. The general curvature expression, redshift-frame conventions and analytic checks are in [first principles](first-principles.md).

Positive expansion H does not fix the sign of q. Present acceleration q(0)<0 differs from acceleration in some earlier interval. In flat matter+CPL dark energy within general relativity, `q0=1/2 + 3 w0(1-Omega_m)/2`; that conversion includes matter/gravity/energy-content assumptions. Excluding the cosmological-constant point `(w0,wa)=(-1,0)` is not excluding q0<0. Constant-w fits to different probes also are not local measurements of w at those probes' effective redshifts.

Uncalibrated SN distances leave a common absolute-magnitude/H0 offset free. The BAO likelihood here uses a free H0 rd scale and does not impose a CMB sound-horizon calibration. CMB reference chains have additional early-universe assumptions. Marginalizing an SN magnitude offset removes its direct H0 degeneracy, but **does not remove H0 from the Gyr clock used to calculate stellar/progenitor evolution**.

There is an exact identification limit: for two distance histories, an arbitrary function `Delta M(z)=5 log10[dL1(z)/dL2(z)]` can exchange their predicted apparent magnitudes. This is not evidence that nature uses that function. It establishes why a model-independent acceleration claim cannot survive completely unconstrained luminosity evolution. Physically informative population and selection constraints are required.

## Matched ages: what survives modern corrections?

The [age investigation](investigations/age-signal.md) approximately recovers the original summary-data relations: R19 around −.062 mag/Gyr and G11 around −.046 after its fitted young-host redshift correction. The latter is itself estimated from the sample and has unreleased shared correction uncertainty. Park uses a further quality-cut sample and a different young-host correction; substituting modern light-curve parameters would silently change its analysis.

On identical modern matched SNe, fully corrected slopes are about −.006 with .012–.014 uncertainty using published tabulated errors. The actual total-covariance diagonal gives about −.007 to −.009 with .011 uncertainty; a simple full-covariance latent-age model gives a profile optimum −.012 and approximate 95% profile interval [−.032,+.010] mag/Gyr. Joint age/redshift/mass/colour sensitivity remains weak, approximately −.015 with profile interval [−.038,+.008]. These are different statistical models, not estimates to average. The profiles are likelihood intervals, not posterior credible intervals. Age PDFs, age/mass/dust correlations and selection remain imperfectly specified by the public summary tables.

Reversing the *entire* bias correction strengthens the relation. It does not isolate the effect of removing a scalar mass step. Cumulative zHD cuts reproduce published counts exactly (100,144,176,192,196); their slopes are approximately reproduced, but the cuts overlap heavily. A paired cut-contrast diagnostic includes zero. This sequence is not five independent detections of a physical dilution law.

A consequential input issue is that the covariance diagonal is not the tabulated distance-error column: their RMS difference is .06037 mag. We therefore preserve both the approximate paper-style regressions and explicit covariance sensitivities. The fully corrected matched ages neither establish a full additional −.030 mag/Gyr law nor prove the absence of all cosmologically relevant evolution.

## The dilution mock and the host-to-progenitor transformation

The published Chung reply's mock assigns age-dependent magnitudes and then forces equal residual means across redshift bins. That operation attenuates a slope by the ratio of within-bin to total age variance. We derive the identity and verify it in paired simulations. Subtracting a **fixed correct** LCDM distance baseline does not require that bin centering.

The strongest counterexample to an overbroad criticism is equally important: actually fitting a flexible redshift baseline can absorb a real evolving luminosity component. The independent auditor constructs such an example. The mock thus does not establish inevitable dilution from the definition of a Hubble residual, and our criticism does not establish that real fitted cosmologies cannot hide evolution. A selection-aware injection through the real joint fitting pipeline is the missing discriminating test.

For a common stable affine mapping between host and progenitor age, compressing the age evolution steepens the inferred luminosity slope inversely, so their product can remain invariant. Broad scatter alone need not defeat this cancellation. But nonlinear mappings, population-dependent offsets, inferred-age error and changing selection can break single-slope transport. Moreover, a law linear in age predicts a consistently weighted **mean** luminosity; substituting a population median requires further justification. These statements and explicit counterexamples are in the [mapping investigation](investigations/mapping-physics.md).

The cosmic-SFH×smooth-DTD calculation reproduces Son's correction scale: under the disclosed B13/C14/LCDM70 convention, median delay changes by 5.29037 Gyr to z=1, giving .158711 mag for a .030 mag/Gyr coefficient. Alternative quadrature confirms it. Under the short-cutoff W26 DTD, that same explicit B13 convention gives 2.42080 Gyr rather than the paper's approximately 1.5 Gyr. Other clocks/CSFH choices change it, but the exact discrepancy remains unresolved; it is not attributed to an author arithmetic error.

The supplied W22 simulation supports much shorter typical delays in its selected host population, but does not identify the W26 paper's exact selection/configuration. Murakami's TITAN multiband host modelling improves the age/SFH evidence; its cosmological-bias estimate is a projection using an existing slope, not a released new TITAN residual likelihood. Its 6,983-host posterior catalogue and finalized calibrated residuals were unavailable at the checked endpoints. Park-style age-removes-mass-step asymmetry can also occur in a common-driver simulation with no direct age-to-luminosity effect. None of these tests identifies age, metallicity or dust uniquely as the causal variable.

## Conditional cosmological results

We first reproduce standard baselines: Pantheon+ flat LCDM gives Omega_m=.3323±.0182, and flat CPL Pantheon+ plus DESI BAO gives q0=−.436±.081. No SH0ES calibrator likelihood is added. The full input matrices, priors, marginal offset treatment and independent checks are in the [cosmology report](investigations/cosmology.md).

The fixed median comparison approximately reproduces Son's BAO+SN q0=.064±.070. Its SN-only q0=−.072±.096 versus −.104±.091 fails our preregistered .2-published-sigma agreement criterion and is retained as a discrepancy. A fixed clock, mean/median convention, exact self-consistent correction and uncertainty prescription are unresolved author-pipeline differences. The full corrected CMB likelihood and reported >9-sigma departure from LCDM were **not** reproduced; no corrected author chain/configuration bundle was available. Reference DESI BAO+CMB CPL chains give q0=.088±.213 and sampled acceleration probability about .33, illustrating why present sign and LCDM rejection are different statistics.

The common slope uncertainty is one shared latent parameter, not an independent error added to every SN. It still does not marginalize all SFH, DTD, calibration, selection and transport uncertainty. Changing the DTD while holding .030 fixed is a stated physical-law sensitivity, not a consistently transformed empirical host slope. The broad-amplitude posterior is deliberately prior-dependent and is not a measured correction.

Smooth kinematic and five-bin q(z) analyses on standard distances also favour low-redshift acceleration. The smooth fit gives q0=−.486±.073. A weakly smoothed first bin (0<z<.1) is −.326±.134; after the fixed median correction it is +.018±.135. The first-bin average is not a point measurement at z=0. Within that finite family, constraining all bins nonnegative worsens chi-square by 116.26 on standard distances and 14.30 after the correction. This boundary comparison is not converted into a nominal sigma, and the poorly identified high-z unconstrained optimum touches a bound. Present-sign sensitivity does not imply no acceleration ever occurred.

## Dust, calibration and current independent-sample evidence

The [standardization investigation](investigations/standardization-dust.md) reproduces native original-DES versus Dovekie flat-LCDM profile estimates, Omega_m=.35195 versus .33032. These releases use different matrix formats: original systematic covariance requires its statistical diagonal; Dovekie provides packed total **precision**. We reconstruct, order and subset them correctly. Matched swaps show interacting distance and covariance effects. The published F99-only intermediate shift is opposite to the total shift, so the total revision cannot be attributed solely to a dust law or solely to calibration from the final vectors.

There are real historical implementation revisions, including F99 approximation and calibration-systematic weighting. The September public low-z host-mass reconstruction changes 114 rows. A serious source discrepancy remains: Hoyt Appendix F describes a **negative** mean shift, whereas Roy Choudhury's equation and public new-minus-old vector are **positive**. We reproduce the positive public vector exactly and use it in the table above. Without Hoyt's executed vector, this is not a verified reproduction of Hoyt's private correction or cosmology. The sign discrepancy is preserved rather than reconciled by assumption.

Dust and intrinsic colour are confounded in optical fits. A constructive latent-colour transformation can move an age-correlated contribution between intrinsic colour and reddening without changing the observables; that demonstrates identification limits, not the true dust distribution. A mixed-geometry calculation also shows why galaxy attenuation RV need not equal SN sightline extinction RV. These distinctions refute a direct equivalence argument, but do not validate a dust-only model.

The latest [Ginolin ZTF v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06799v3), revised 17 September, is substantial conditional counterevidence to a universal dust-only account. It reports an intrinsic global-mass step .103±.018 mag after allowing environment-dependent dust distributions. ZTF provides separate survey photometry; shared SED training/calibration remains. Its proxies are mass and local/global colour, not measured ages. Exact final masks and per-SN BayeSN posterior products are absent, and the authors identify calibration limitations that preclude treating this as a completed acceleration measurement. These are source-verified author results, not locally reproduced BayeSN fits. See the [release and revision audit](investigations/ztf-source-update.md).

The September Kim and Kelsey papers add age-indicator and host-SED evidence but do not release a new independent age-corrected acceleration likelihood. A light-curve-shape/age relation can be relevant to standardization without proving a residual luminosity trend after standardization. Joint spectroscopic metallicity, dust and age posteriors are needed to identify a metallicity contribution independently; insignificant marginal/subgroup differences do not prove its absence.

## Directional cosmography is a separate branch

The [directional investigation](investigations/directional-audit.md) distinguishes corrected-distance C1 fits from the principal latent light-curve C2 likelihood, redshift frames, intrinsic distributions and the imposed age correction. C1 baseline values and likelihood normalization are reproduced. A controlled exact-LCDM injection gives a cubic-expansion q bias about +.0264 under the tested sample weighting, far smaller than the roughly +.35 age-correction shift. A generic Taylor-truncation objection therefore does not explain the entire shift.

Current primary-source checking found that Ray's subsequently revised paper acknowledges an Equatorial/Galactic coordinate error and withdraws the original large deceleration values after reanalysis. The authors also cannot recover the original full-sample result, which the coordinate error alone cannot explain. Its simplified likelihood differs from Sah's full latent covariance model. Thus neither the obsolete Ray result nor its revision should be treated as a reproduction of the principal C2 calculation. The final directional report records the bounded numerical reproduction and precise remaining differences. Dipole improvement, the isotropic q sign and a covariant physical anisotropy interpretation are distinct claims; an unidentified dipole scale under the null precludes an automatic Wilks sigma conversion.

## Numerical trust and remaining limits

Consequential calculations passed analytic distance limits, Astropy comparisons, covariance-order/positivity checks and direct versus accelerated likelihood checks. An independent Astra Ultra auditor used a different distance ODE, direct Cholesky intercept treatment and alternate age integration: joint chi-square agrees within 5.3e−7 at the tested modes. It found no consequential numerical bug in the certified central calculations. This is computational evidence on shared data, not independent astrophysical confirmation.

Two hundred conditional Gaussian injections recover their input matter density within the simulation's resolution, with empirical scatter matching fitted uncertainty and 69% one-SD coverage. Independent MCMC repeats quantify Monte Carlo variability. Exact zeros/ones in sampled sign fractions are not exact posterior probabilities. Source snapshots and authoritative replay resolve an initially inadequate code-at-completion hashing scheme; earlier outputs and the failed coarse interpolation check remain labelled historical.

One model check fails usefully: observed quadratic residual scatter is smaller than the simple fixed-covariance Gaussian generator predicts (roughly chi-square 1404–1406 versus 1589 expected, lower-tail probability a few 10^-4). It affects corrected and uncorrected variants. Conservative systematic covariance, selection and non-Gaussian release conventions are possible explanations, not established diagnoses. We retain this underdispersion and do not rescale errors to hide it. Inference uncertainty is conditional on the release likelihood; numerical convergence cannot establish physical completeness.

## Most informative next evidence

A decisive advance would combine independent host information with held-out distance prediction, rather than another fit of the same corrected magnitudes. Measure local spectra plus UV–MIR host SEDs, SN optical/NIR colour, sightline dust constraints and metallicity across redshift; retain full joint posteriors and detection/host-follow-up selection. Fit competing intrinsic-age, dust and joint models to a training population, then preregister within-redshift and across-redshift predictions for separate survey objects/calibration partitions. Run injections through the actual photometry-to-selection-to-cosmology pipeline with populations and cosmology jointly refit. Recovery only validates the injected assumptions; successful held-out predictions add empirical information.

The most immediately useful releases are exact matched-age crosswalks/posteriors and correction covariance; W26/C26 selected host-delay distributions and mock configurations; Son's executed correction/clock/covariance/full-likelihood bundle; TITAN's SFH and calibrated residual products; Hoyt's signed executed vector; and ZTF's final masks plus BayeSN posterior/run products. [Precise unsent requests](author-data-requests.md) specify the products and what each would decide.

The defensible stopping point is therefore a bounded scientific result: material astrophysical sensitivity is demonstrated; several simplistic justifications on both sides fail; a unique transported age correction and a robust present-deceleration claim remain unidentified by the available public evidence. Exact-reproduction barriers, informative failures and conditional results remain part of the deliverable. No authors were contacted and nothing was published or uploaded.
